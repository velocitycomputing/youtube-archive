## Summary

The US has achieved a breakthrough in reopening the Strait of Hormuz after degrading Iran's coastal military infrastructure, providing direct ship protection, and enabling expensive shuttle-run transfers of oil. Oil exports from the Gulf have recovered to pre-war levels (16.5 million barrels per day by September), marking a dramatic reversal from months of blockade. However, Iran has responded by escalating attacks on shipping, recording the highest number of weekly incidents since the war began, including a strike on the Indian tanker *On Peace* and a concerning attack on the *Aces* tanker 50 nautical miles north of Qatar—far deeper into the Persian Gulf than previous attacks. This week alone, crude flow through Hormuz collapsed 27% to 10.1 million barrels per day (74% of pre-war levels), suggesting the US victory may be fragile.

For you: Shipping costs and energy prices will likely remain elevated if this conflict persists, as shuttle runs charge $40 million per operation with sailor bonuses at $25,000. Iran's economy is imploding (currency halved in value, $2 billion in oil revenue missing), but the regime shows no sign of backing down—IRGC advisers have floated extending the conflict through 2029. Watch for whether Iran escalates attacks deeper into the Persian Gulf, which would require a much larger permanent US naval presence, and monitor for internal Iranian political shifts as economic desperation pressures even hardliners toward diplomacy.
