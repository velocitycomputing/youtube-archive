## Summary

The video follows a professional climber, who has no parkour background, as he tries to learn the Brighton wall run. It's a famous parkour challenge: you run diagonally across a concrete wall above a flight of stairs and land on hard concrete. His coach is Toby, a veteran parkour athlete from the Storror team. The first session starts on low-risk ground and uses crash mats. Toby teaches the basics: how to build run-up speed, how to lean into the wall and use friction rather than fighting it, and how to push off at the end. He also teaches a few other moves, including the speed step, the cat pass and the arm jump (cat leap). The climber's first attempts go poorly. He slips, loses commitment partway across, and stacks it once, and he gets shaken and takes a break. His body is sore by the end. Two days later he comes back in worse physical shape. He drills the run, starts to feel "casual" on the wall, and then removes the mats. Toby suggests stamping the ground to settle his nerves, and he calls on the mental skills from competition climbing to push through the fear. After several hesitant tries he completes the wall run on concrete. The landing isn't clean and his ankle takes a bit of a knock, but he's fine. They compare the wall run to Rainbow Rocket in climbing and call it a top-tier iconic challenge.

For you, the useful points are about technique and mindset, with a safety caveat. Technique: run faster and commit to the speed along the wall. Lean into the wall and trust the friction instead of stopping partway. Shorten your steps, and keep your chest up and square. Look at your final mark and push off at the end. Absorb the landing with your legs, not stiffly. Wear grippy shoes, and watch for green algae or moss, which parkour people call "getting greened". Progress in stages: start with low-stakes versions and mats, then remove the mats once the movement feels automatic. On nerves, stamp the ground hard to settle your nervous system, and take a break when your head goes. If you try a version of this yourself, learn the basics first (the speed step, cat pass and arm jump are the ones the video covers). Don't start on a real wall run, since the video notes it isn't beginner-friendly and the climber was sore for days. Warm up, and expect your legs and ankles to take a beating.
