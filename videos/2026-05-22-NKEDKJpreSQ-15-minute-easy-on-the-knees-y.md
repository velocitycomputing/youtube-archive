## Summary

Joe Wicks, on Body Coach TV, presents a 15-minute low-impact “easy on the knees” workout built around 15 moves using a 40-seconds-work, 20-seconds-rest format. The routine emphasizes controlled, no-jump movements with the foot staying flat, aiming to strengthen the quads, hamstrings, glutes, core, obliques, shoulders, and balance systems while limiting knee stress. Named exercises include cross-body knee raises, squat steps, hamstring touch-back lunges, curtsy lunges, sumo squat holds, one-leg stands with lateral raises, standing punches, high-plank knee-to-chest movements, slow squats, torso twists, and step punches with heel kicks. Key claims are that gentle, controlled knee bending can help maintain joint function and reduce stiffness, that strengthening the legs stabilizes the knee, and that people should reduce depth, skip moves, or use scaled versions—such as shallow squats, holding a plank, or doing planks from the knees—rather than forcing full range of motion. Wicks also promotes the Body Coach app for training, diet, and nutrition support, mentioning a free week trial.

Actionable takeaways: this is a practical low-impact strength and mobility session for beginners, people with knee discomfort, or anyone avoiding jumping, but it should be treated as a starting point rather than medical treatment. If using it for knee pain, keep pain mild, avoid sharp pain, swelling, locking, or instability, and reduce squat depth, reps, or range until tolerance improves; persistent or worsening symptoms warrant a clinician or physical therapist evaluation. Useful research leads include low-impact lower-body strengthening for knee osteoarthritis or patellofemoral pain, unilateral leg strength, core stability, and balance training. Concrete decisions to consider: try it 1–3 times per week, track pain before/during/after, choose modifications when needed, progress by adding depth, reps, slower tempo, or balance challenges, and decide whether the Body Coach app or a more individualized rehab-style program is needed. Follow-up questions: Is the knee pain mechanical, inflammatory, or activity-related? Are there asymmetries between legs? Can wrist or plank positions be modified? Is the goal general fitness, knee rehab, or return to higher-impact activity?
