## Summary

The video follows a steak expert visiting four stores—Costco, Whole Foods, Wild Fork, and Walmart—to purchase and evaluate their most expensive ribeyes. At each location, he demonstrates what to look for when selecting quality beef: marbling distribution, the size of the ribeye "cap" (the most flavorful part), and avoiding large globs of fat that won't render properly. He highlights that Costco had no prime steaks available, explains that flash-frozen beef from Wild Fork ($22) performs as well as fresh when thawed properly, and notes that even within the same store, steak quality varies significantly between packages. After simple seasoning (salt, pepper, garlic powder) and cooking, blind taste tests reveal the Costco choice-grade steak (~$29) rated highest, the Walmart steak ($15) delivered exceptional value, the Whole Foods premium steak ($50) disappointed, and the Wild Fork aged prime ($22) performed excellently.

Buy steaks based on marbling pattern and cap size, not grade alone—choice-grade with great marbling beats prime with poor distribution. Compare all available packages even from the same store; don't assume they're identical. Establish a relationship with a knowledgeable butcher who can guide you to the best cut for your cooking method. Costco and Walmart offer better value than premium grocers if you know what to evaluate, and flash-frozen beef is a legitimate option. Keep seasoning minimal to let steak quality shine, and pair it with proper sides like roasted potatoes to complete the meal.
