## Summary

Scott Galloway, an NYU Stern professor and author of the new book "Notes on Being a Man," tells Ross Douthat why he believes young American men are in crisis. He traces his concern to Alex Kearns, a young Robinhood trader who died by suicide in 2020 after an erroneous message said he owed thousands of dollars. Galloway also says he saw young men go unmentioned at the Democratic convention. He frames his own rise as the product of an America that "loved the unremarkable": he got into UCLA on appeal, graduated with a 2.27 GPA, and still got into Berkeley's graduate school. He argues that infrastructure has been lost. Schools favor girls' behavior, since boys' prefrontal cortices lag by about 18 months. Shop classes and trades on-ramps have vanished. Elite colleges artificially restrict admissions, and non-dischargeable student debt falls on dropouts. He rejects "toxic masculinity" as a phrase and defines healthy masculinity through three Ps: provider, protector and procreator. He says young men should be allowed to be motivated by wanting a mate, and he calls the incel movement an excuse. He says women are drawn to signaled resources, intelligence and humor, and above all kindness. Douthat presses him on whether his prescriptions are really male-specific, and on the risks from stepfathers and non-parental mentors. Galloway concludes that the single point of failure for boys is losing a male role model, so the fix "comes from you and me." The transcript was truncated at about 30,000 of 41,000 characters, so the final part of the conversation isn't covered.

For you, the practical takeaways are mostly things to do yourself or push for locally. Galloway's proposals are: start boys in kindergarten at six ("redshirting"); pay teachers better and recruit more men into primary schools; compensate after-school sports coaches; and create mandatory national service for ages 18 to 22, such as the military, smokejumping or elder care. He also wants more "third places" such as church, clubs and sports, where people can meet outside of dating apps. On a personal level, if you have sons or young men in your life, be an active male mentor or role model, especially for boys in single-parent homes, and don't hold back out of fear of suspicion. Help them build an economic plan (a trade, a degree or other work), and encourage kindness and the habit of laughing, which he says matter in relationships. Since he stresses that a boy's outcomes depend heavily on the men around him, volunteering as a coach, Scout leader or mentor is the most concrete step he points to.
