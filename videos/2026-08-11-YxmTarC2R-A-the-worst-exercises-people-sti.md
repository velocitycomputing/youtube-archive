## Summary

The video argues that five common exercises are inferior to alternatives, mostly on the basis of muscle-growth biomechanics (full range of motion, loading the muscle at long lengths, and training both sides efficiently). The plank is an isometric hold with no concentric or eccentric work, so he replaces it with the ab rollout, which has a large range of motion and peak tension at the stretched position. The Superman (back extension from the floor) has only a few inches of motion and can't be loaded, so he replaces it with a flexion row done with cables, dumbbells, or a barbell. The rack pull is usually done from mid-thigh, which cuts the range of motion and lets you use ego-sized loads. He says it only makes sense for some bodybuilders (and then from at least knee height) or as powerlifting assistance work, where deficit deadlifts transfer better. His replacement is the snatch-grip deficit deadlift, done for sets of about 15 reps from a high-hip, rounded-back start, with the back unrounding under control. The tricep kickback puts its hardest resistance in the shortened position, so he swaps it for the dumbbell skull crusher, which is hardest at the stretched bottom. The one-arm-at-a-time dumbbell press fatigues the arm that is holding the weight, so he recommends a regular two-arm dumbbell press, or a chest-press or Hammer Strength machine if you want unilateral work. He also recommends following Naja Strength on Instagram.

For action, swap each exercise as follows: plank to ab rollout, Superman to cable, dumbbell, or barbell row, rack pull to snatch-grip deficit deadlift (or at least a below-knee rack pull if you keep it), tricep kickback to dumbbell skull crusher, and single-arm dumbbell press to two-arm dumbbell press or a machine press. For the rollout, use the whole range, control the lowering phase, and stop where you can still return. For the snatch-grip deficit deadlift, start light enough for about 15 controlled reps and do the reps slowly. His reasoning is that moderate-to-high reps through a deep range of motion, with the most load where the muscle is stretched, grow muscle best. Treat this as one coach's opinion, since the video cites no studies. The isometric claim is overstated: planks do train the abs and are low-risk. The video's idea that rack pulls are never useful is also stronger than the evidence supports. The snatch-grip deficit deadlift with a deliberately rounded back is a high-fatigue, higher-risk lift, so use light weight and good form, and skip it if you have back problems.
