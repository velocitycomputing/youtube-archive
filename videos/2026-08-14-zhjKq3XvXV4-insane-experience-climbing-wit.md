## Summary

A climber (Magnus) spends a day in the forest with Leo, a French "European Tarzan" who moves through trees barefoot and without ropes. They start with a cold-weather warm-up of slow, flowing movement around a sturdy tree, which is meant to build trust in the branches. They then practice a quadrupedal technique: hands and feet on the branch, small steps, a slight knee bend, relaxed scapula, eyes forward instead of on the hands. Leo then leads Magnus up roughly 15–20 m. There he transfers between trees using thin, flexible trunks and a knee-bar. Magnus is scared and says so repeatedly. He eventually crosses a gap himself and works back down along a large oak branch. Leo's repeated advice is "don't think, breathe," trust the tree's strength, grab the central trunk rather than side branches, and look around and try to save yourself if you fall. Magnus brings a rope and harness as a hidden backup but never uses them. He ends the day with a sense of accomplishment and says Leo's guidance is the only reason he tried it.

For you, the useful part is the technique, not the stunt. Practice on a low, sturdy tree first so you learn how much a branch flexes before it breaks. Keep your arms straight and load your legs. Use small steps, and move on all fours when a branch is thin. Hold the central trunk, keep two points of contact, and breathe through the scary moments. Don't copy the 15–20 m tree-to-tree transfers. They were done without ropes, in cold, mossy conditions, by someone with years of practice, and Leo himself said trees are weaker outside summer. If you want to try this, go with an experienced guide as Magnus did. Wear a rope and harness for anything high, as Magnus brought. Don't go barefoot unless your feet are conditioned, since Magnus said his were getting sore.
