## Summary

Buttigieg, interviewed by Jonathan Martin over lunch in Traverse City, Michigan, argued that Democrats shouldn't run on "returning to normal." He said the party risks an unspoken pull back to 2012, 1992, or 2021, and that he is purging "re-" and "back" words from his vocabulary, including "build back better." He said the 2020 Biden approach would have worked if it could have, and that dismantled institutions like USAID and the Education Department shouldn't simply be rebuilt as they were. He declined to say how he voted in Michigan's Senate primary, saying he wants to help unify the party afterward. He called Republican Mike Rogers a tough general-election opponent, said he briefly considered running for Senate or governor but felt too new to Michigan, and praised Mayor Mamdani's focus on delivery (potholes, snow) while warning that big promises like freezing rent can catch up with you. On 2028, he said he is the same person but now arrives in states like South Carolina with things he delivered as Transportation Secretary. He said the "carve up constituencies" style of politics peaked in 2019–2020, tied his story to a theme of belonging that he expects to matter more in the AI era, and described the incident that separated him from his kids as a symptom of toxic politics. He argued that 2028 should be a chance to "turn the page" on Trump rather than just run against him.
