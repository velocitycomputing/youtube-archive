## Summary

Roy Z (a shredder guitarist) and the host talked about building and keeping guitar speed. Roy's daily warm-up is a "1-4-3-4" finger pattern across four frets, which he shifts down a fret and varies as 1-4-2-4. It also works as a warm-up test: if he can't play it cleanly the moment he picks up the guitar, he needs about 15 minutes before he's ready. His main speed method is "speed bursts." You set the metronome about 10 BPM above what you can play cleanly and accept some sloppiness, aiming for roughly 80% accuracy, or 7–8 of every 10 reps good. The right level is the "almost got it, one more take" feeling. After a week, the old tempo feels easier and looser. He says this works best once you already have some speed, because beginners are better off with the slow-and-gradually-faster approach. It also plateaus, and gains slow down after the early quick wins. He isn't sure whether starting late caps your top speed, but he says it mostly takes much more time and practice, and adults have less of that. He says 16th notes at 140 BPM already sounds fast to most listeners, and that many players stall at 120. Once you push your baseline above that, it holds even after weeks off. His own baseline is about 180, with a goal of 200 and a best of about 220. They then did endurance tests of one minute of chromatic playing at 140 and 170 BPM, and Roy couldn't hold 219 BPM consistently. They closed on the point that speed isn't the goal, but it gives you options so technique doesn't limit the music you want to play. Melody comes first, and you have to enjoy the metronome work.

For you, start with a 1-4-3-4 / 1-4-2-4 exercise as a warm-up, and use it as a readiness check before pushing speed. Find a tempo where you're stable, then set the metronome about 10 BPM faster for around 20 minutes a day. Aim for about 80% clean takes rather than sloppy playing for its own sake. At the end of the week, drop back 10 BPM and check whether that tempo feels easier. If it isn't working, record a video and check your accuracy level, since Roy says most "it's not working" cases come from practicing it wrong or not long enough. Don't use this method if you're a beginner. Set a baseline goal of 16th notes at 140 BPM, and if you're stuck near 120, this is the method to try. Use legato to give your picking hand a break when your pick slips. Practice with melodic lines and exercises you enjoy, so speed ends up serving the music you want to play.
