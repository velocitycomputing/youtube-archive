## Summary

Ian Bremmer and the host discussed UN General Assembly week, Antonio Guterres’s outgoing tenure, the Security Council’s paralysis, and the UN’s remaining value in a “Gzero” world of 193 member states and more than 8 billion people. Bremmer praised Guterres’s integrity and credited him with brokering the Black Sea grain deal, which he said mattered more for global-south food security than many better-known conflicts, and with launching a high-level AI panel that led to the Global Summit of the Future recommendations and an international scientific panel on AI, reportedly supported by every country except Russia and run by Yoshua Bengio and Maria Ressa. He named Trump, Putin, Xi, Mark Carney, Pope Leo, the Houthis, Elon Musk/SpaceX, China, the US, Canada, EU, UK, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, World Food Program, WHO, IMF, World Bank, and USAID, while claiming US aid cuts and hostility weaken the UN, China seeks influence in multilateral bodies, Trump’s “Board of Peace” failed, and small/non-state actors increasingly raise the cost of great-power escalation, citing Venezuela/Maduro, Ukraine, Iran, and Taiwan.

For the user, the practical takeaway is to treat UN coverage less as a verdict on effectiveness and more as a risk dashboard: track World Food Program funding, WHO outbreak reporting, humanitarian access in Ukraine/Sudan/Yemen/Gaza/Afghanistan, and US aid cuts as leading indicators for food insecurity, disease spread, and migration. Research leads include the UN AI scientific panel, China’s push to host AI centers and staff UN agencies, Security Council reform/veto debates, and the role of non-state actors such as SpaceX and the Houthis in shaping deterrence. Decisions to consider: monitor whether the next secretary-general is independent, whether the US continues paying dues and supporting agencies, and whether AI governance recommendations produce binding standards; follow-up questions should ask how Gzero dynamics change corporate exposure to sanctions, supply-chain shocks, and geopolitical escalation, and what health or humanitarian metrics would signal that UN agencies are becoming too weak to manage a crisis.
