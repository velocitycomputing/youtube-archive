## Summary

Demetri Pervich, chairman of Silverado Policy Accelerator, argued on Global Gambit that Ukraine is entering a precarious phase because Russia has improved strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, has the initiative across most of the front, and enjoys advantages in manpower and standardized drone production, while Ukraine’s recruitment, training, and mobilization remain inadequate. He cited Zelensky’s visit to a counterattack near Kupyansk despite Russian Defense Minister Andrei Belousov’s claim that Kupyansk was taken, Russian infiltration tactics that make the front line porous, Russia’s continued out-recruiting of Ukraine—including contracts offered to arrestees—and Zelensky’s decision to let young men leave, with tens or hundreds of thousands reportedly fleeing, as evidence of strategic mismanagement. Pervich said Ukraine’s main bright spot is deep strike—especially Flamingo-type cruise missiles and drones—but it needs longer range and larger payloads against Moscow and St. Petersburg to create reciprocal deterrence against Russian energy attacks. He also said Russia’s economy is under strain, with Urals oil below $50, liquid assets in the National Wealth Fund depleting, high inflation, high interest rates, labor shortages, and a GDP increasingly dominated by weapons production that is destroyed in Ukraine, though he did not expect imminent collapse because of competent management by central bank head Elvira Nabiullina. He viewed Putin as unlikely to negotiate for 12–18 months because he believes he has the initiative, receives inflated battlefield reports from commanders such as Valery Gerasimov, sees Zelensky weakened by corruption scandals, Andriy Yermak’s departure, domestic opposition, and Trump pressure, and regards Europe as irrelevant unless it actually rearms. Pervich dismissed the 28-point peace plan as unrealistic because Russia would reject Trump as sole peace-council chairman, use of frozen Russian central-bank reserves for reconstruction, and caps on Ukrainian forces, and he expected a continuing attritional status quo, possible limited energy truce, adjustable Russian objectives ranging from Donbas to Odesa, Kherson, Mykolaiv, and broader “Novorossiya,” and a war unlikely to remain at current intensity past 2027.
