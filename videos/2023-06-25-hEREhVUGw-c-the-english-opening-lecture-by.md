## Summary

GM Ben Finegold's sponsored lecture introduces the English Opening (1.c4) and walks through it by way of six annotated games by GM Yasser Seirawan, who plays it almost exclusively. He explains that 1.c4 transposes widely, for example into the Queen's Gambit, Nimzo-Indian, Grünfeld, Caro-Kann or Slav structures. This is why some players avoid it, and why White's opponents often know it less well than they know 1.e4 and 1.d4. The games show several approaches:
- **Reversed Sicilian vs. Tal:** Seirawan fianchettoes, plays d3, a3 and b4-b5, and trades his bishop for the knight so he keeps a good knight against a bad bishop. He then wins a long opposite-colored-bishop endgame.
- **vs. Korchnoi:** In the 1.c4 Nf6 2.Nc3 e6 3.e4 line, White keeps a lead in development and avoids trades.
- **vs. Dreev:** This is a Slav/Caro-Kann-type setup. Seirawan wins a pawn with Qa4+ and converts it.
- **vs. Dzindzichashvili:** The line is 1.c4 c5 2.g3 with Bg2 and a delayed Nf3. Early pressure on the long diagonal and on b7 leads to a crushing win.
- **vs. Ivanov:** After 1.c4 Nf6 2.Nc3 e5 3.e3, Seirawan plays a Bxa6 sacrifice and a rook lift. Finegold doubts the recorded final moves are accurate.
- **vs. Miles:** The transcript is truncated in the 1.c4 c5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.e4 Nb4 line, so that game is incomplete.

For your own play, the lecture suggests several things. If you play White and are tired of facing mainstream e4/d4 theory, 1.c4 is a practical weapon, since opponents are less prepared against it. Delay Nf3 when you want the g2 bishop's long diagonal and pressure on d5 and b7.

Study Seirawan's games, especially the Tal and Dreev games, for the typical plans: trade your bishop for a knight to get a good knight against a bad bishop, play on the queenside with b4-b5 and Rc1, and use the half-open c-file. If you play Black, you can reach the same structures by choosing solid replies like ...e6 or ...c6, and Finegold notes that ...c5 against the Korchnoi line fell out of favor. Expect to learn a lot of transpositional theory, since the lecture only samples the variations. Don't trust the Ivanov game score at face value.
