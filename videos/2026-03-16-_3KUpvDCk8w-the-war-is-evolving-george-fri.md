## Summary

George Friedman and Christian Smith, in a Geopolitical Futures podcast, argued that the US-Israeli war with Iran, described as two and a half weeks old, began with aims to neutralize Iran’s nuclear program and force regime change, but evolved into a struggle against the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), which they said was the real governing, military, and economic power and survived decapitation of the civilian government. Friedman claimed the strategy rested on intelligence and military miscalculations: that destroying civilian leaders would trigger an uprising, that air power alone could compel capitulation, and that Iran’s decentralized “Mosaic Force” structure would prevent rapid collapse. He cited continued Iranian missile and drone attacks, including toward Turkey and Incirlik, the IRGC’s ability to threaten or close the Strait of Hormuz, roughly 900 pounds of enriched uranium as an unresolved nuclear issue, and oil-reserve releases by the international energy administration as factors shaping global energy risk. He also discussed Trump’s anti-ground-war pledge, Pete Hegseth, mission creep, Gulf states’ unwillingness to absorb regional burden, and the secular Artesh army as a possible unengaged counterweight to the IRGC.

Actionable takeaways are to treat the Strait of Hormuz, IRGC drone and missile inventory, and Artesh behavior as the key variables for energy, shipping, and geopolitical risk rather than relying on official claims that Iranian military capability is destroyed. Monitor tanker transits, war-risk insurance premiums, Brent/WTI price moves, oil-reserve releases, and statements from Gulf states and Turkey; research the IRGC’s decentralized command, economic network, and Artesh-IRGC rivalry as leads for whether the conflict can be resolved by air power, negotiation, proxy pressure, or ground forces. For decisions, stress-test portfolios and budgets for sustained oil-price shocks, avoid assuming a quick de-escalation, and ask follow-up questions: Are Iranian strikes declining because of attrition or hoarding? Can Hormuz be reopened without ground troops? Will the Artesh act against the IRGC? Is the administration seeking uranium control, IRGC destruction, or a negotiated exit?
