## Summary

Bobby Stuckey, a Master Sommelier and co-owner of Frasca restaurant, shares how his early success as a busboy—thriving in high-chaos, high-demand environments despite being ADD and dyslexic—shaped his philosophy on hospitality. He distinguishes between service (the mechanics of proper technique) and hospitality (making guests feel genuinely welcomed), and shows how excelling at both requires obsessive attention to detail: detailed pre-shift meetings with clear job descriptions for every position, personal wine tastings where he coaches others through the blind six-wine tasting exam, and a relentless standard that every night's execution matters more than past achievements. His path to the Master Sommelier diploma involved years of failure, doubt from peers, and the struggle of holding two distinct visions—his wife Danette's focus on food connection and his own on wine connection—while opening a second restaurant, Tavernetta, and growing his team from 12 to 86 people.

The actionable reality: mastery demands sustained, exhausting effort with no guaranteed endpoint. Bobby works 13-hour days, nights, weekends, and holidays with minimal time for family, and his advice is simply to focus on today's performance, take it one step at a time, and accept that nothing worth achieving arrives quickly. If you're pursuing something demanding in hospitality, creative work, or any craft where excellence compounds, prepare for prolonged failure, expect peers to question whether it's worth the cost, and understand that the discipline is the reward—not a future milestone.
