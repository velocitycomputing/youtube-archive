## Summary

A leaked 28-point White House draft peace plan for Ukraine is analyzed as a bad deal for Ukraine and the United States and a win for Russia and China, because it confirms Ukrainian sovereignty only symbolically, imposes a Russia-Ukraine-Europe non-aggression agreement, freezes NATO expansion, limits Ukraine’s armed forces to 600,000, bars Ukraine from NATO and foreign troops, stations European jets in Poland, and creates a US security guarantee that pays the US and revokes benefits if Russia invades. The draft also offers Ukraine EU eligibility and preferential market access, a World Bank-backed reconstruction and demining package, $100 billion of frozen Russian assets for US-led Ukraine investment with 50% of profits to the US plus $100 billion from Europe, staged sanctions relief and G8 reintegration for Russia, a US-Russia security working group, START extension, IAEA-supervised Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant with 50/50 electricity, language/minority/“Nazi” education provisions, recognition of Crimea, Luhansk and Donetsk as Russian, freezing Kherson and Zaporizhzhia along contact lines, a demilitarized Donetsk buffer, Black Sea grain access, prisoner/children returns, elections in 100 days, full amnesty, and a Trump-headed peace council. The speaker argues the plan may embolden China over Taiwan, notes Russia earns about $66 million a day from oil and could recoup $100 billion in about 4.5 years, and warns that 20,000–35,000 Ukrainian children may have been taken by Russia.

For the user, the actionable steps are to read the full leaked text at rybth.substack.com, compare it with official White House, NATO, EU, Ukrainian, Russian, and IAEA statements, and track whether the plan changes before any ceasefire; treat it as a research lead on deterrence, frozen-asset law, START arms control, EU enlargement, demining, reconstruction finance, and the Taiwan Strait analogy. Concrete follow-up questions are: who pays the US guarantee and how is “compensation” defined; what military response is triggered if Russia invades; who monitors the demilitarized zone and Zaporizhzhia plant; can occupied Ukrainians vote; will amnesty shield commanders and security-service perpetrators; will sanctions actually be reimposed; and will NATO expansion remain legally open. No direct health implications are present, but the practical implications are geopolitical: decisions about supporting Ukraine, opposing NATO-freeze language, demanding war-crime accountability, pushing for child-repatriation transparency, and monitoring whether the plan weakens US credibility with allies and China.
