## Summary

Destin and his team build a rig that mounts two weed eaters facing each other, with a high-speed camera overhead. They run a single-elimination bracket of trimmer lines to see which is toughest, and the loser is whichever line breaks first. The entrants were the Echo Crossfire, a star profile, a triangle, a "sharp square", Razor Core, a blue line, and a grey "blobby" titanium-force line. Crossfire beat Star and Sharp Square beat Triangle. Razor Core beat Blue after Blue frayed and came out an inch shorter. Grey beat a sharp square in a round where the sharp square's line was yanked out of its root. Sharp Square then beat Grey in the East semifinal after a second round, and Razor Core beat Crossfire in the West semifinal. During the semis they noticed that the solid-core line seemed to hold up better. Sharp Square beat Razor Core in the final, when the orange line lost a large chunk after a long, close fight. They concluded that failure comes mainly from how tightly the line bends as it wraps and unwraps. A tight bend radius, around half an inch, creates tensile stress and tears the line apart, so tougher lines are the ones that don't bend as sharply. Destin admits the tournament wasn't scientific. Judging mistakes were found afterward, so the results may be flawed. The episode ends with an Audible sponsor read recommending *The Hobbit*.

For you, the main takeaway is that a sharp-edged square line with a solid core held up best in this informal test, and that lines that wrap tightly and unwrap tear themselves apart. If you're buying trimmer line, that points toward a solid-core, square-profile line over a softer or star-profile one. Treat it as a hint rather than proof, since the creators say the results are potentially flawed.
