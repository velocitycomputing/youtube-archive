## Summary

Brandon Deon argues that learning the fretboard by reciting the chromatic note order (F, F#, G…) only trains you to count, which is too slow for real jamming. Instead he teaches interval shapes that connect every instance of a note, using C (8th fret, low E) as the example. Two strings up and two frets up gives the same note, and from there another two strings up and three frets up gives the next one. This works for the E-D-B strings and the A-G-E strings. Three strings up and down three frets (from low E) or down two frets (from the A string) also lands on the same note. On the same string, add or subtract 12 frets. One string higher is five frets down, except G to B, where it's four because of the B string's tuning. The high E and low E are on the same fret. He notes that the two-strings-up shapes let you play a five-note pentatonic line diagonally instead of in a box. He says that instant note-finding is what lets players target chord tones and play voicings faster, and that this separated him from a better player he once took a lesson from. The last third of the video is a pitch for his 52-Week Guitar Player program.

To put this to use, pick one note, such as C, and practice finding every instance of it across all six strings using only the shapes above, without counting up from the nut. The shapes are: +2 strings/+2 frets, +2 strings/+3 frets (for the E-D-B strings), +3 strings/−3 frets, +1 string/−5 frets (−4 from G to B), and ±12 on the same string. Then repeat with the A string as the starting point (3rd fret C). Then repeat with other notes, ideally while a backing track plays so you find notes in time with the music. Once the shapes are automatic, use the diagonal pentatonic approach to break out of the box patterns, and aim your solo lines at chord tones. You don't need the paid program to do any of this. The video is free, and the sales pitch has a deadline (March 31st, 2026) that has already passed, so the enrollment offer has expired and only the waiting list applies.
