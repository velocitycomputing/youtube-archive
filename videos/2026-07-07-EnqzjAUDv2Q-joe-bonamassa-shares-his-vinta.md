## Summary

Joe Bonamassa showcased his vintage Marshall amplifier collection, tracing the brand's history from its 1960s origins when Ken Bran and Jim Marshall copied Leo Fender's designs using English components. He demonstrated multiple classic models including the 1965 JTM45 (the foundational Marshall sound with 45W tube rectifier and warm, "squishy" tone), a rare 1966 JTM45 bass model formerly owned by Roy Orbison's band member Billy Gilmore, a small box 50 (tighter with EL34 tubes, favored by Jethro Tull's Martin Barre), his personal 1969 100W Super Lead (purchased at age 13 for $375), and the Silver Jubilee 2555. He explained how circuit differences—tube types, transformers, caps, and English construction—define each amp's character, and discussed the decline in tone quality as Marshall added complexity: later models like the JCM900 abandoned Drake transformers and gained master volume controls but lost the headroom and "bloom" that creates the symbiotic relationship between guitar player and amp.

For guitarists interested in vintage Marshall tone: prioritize the JTM45 and early Super Lead models as the sonic benchmark, use a Gibson with JTM45s for optimal pairing and a Strat with Super Leads for clean tone. If shopping for vintage units, authenticate carefully (fake Bluesbreaker combos abound) and verify tube types and transformer quality. For live use, the Silver Jubilee 2555 provides a master volume option without sacrificing the classic Marshall feel. Avoid overcomplicating your setup—the early Plexiglas and metal-panel heads achieve great tone with simple controls, so resist the temptation of later multi-knob designs.
