## Summary

Russia's military recruitment is collapsing relative to casualty rates, with monthly losses exceeding 30,000 troops while recruitment averages only ~1,080 per month—a gap that widens each month and forces the military to lower standards, deploy with minimal training, and cannibalize specialized units like drone operators for frontline infantry roles. To offset this crisis, Russia is aggressively recruiting foreign troops from Africa and Southeast Asia, offering signing bonuses worth an entire lifetime of earnings in countries like the DRC ($13,000 versus annual average income of ~$600), and openly advertising these contracts on websites targeting Cambodia, Zimbabwe, Iraq, and Myanmar. Russia is recruiting approximately 18,000 foreign soldiers—roughly 2–3% of weekly recruitment needs—filling a critical manpower gap.

This recruitment crisis creates a compounding military weakness: each month the Russian army shrinks in effective capacity, forcing it to spread already-thin forces across expanding frontlines while losing trained personnel to high-casualty assault missions. Ukraine and Western allies should pressure source countries to restrict military recruitment, though this faces resistance since remittances are economically valuable and governments view military service as removing unemployed youth. Without stemming recruitment from abroad or dramatically reducing casualties, Russia's manpower advantage will erode predictably each month, eventually forcing either strategic withdrawal or a catastrophic loss of military capability.
