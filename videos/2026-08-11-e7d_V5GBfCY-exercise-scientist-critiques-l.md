## Summary

Dr. Mike (an exercise scientist) and his co-host Scott react to a clip of Lenny Kravitz, age 61, showing his diet and workouts at his Bahamas home. Kravitz shows a fridge and garden full of greens, herbs and tropical fruit, says he is vegan and mostly raw, and sometimes cuts out even fruit sugar. He says he trains 5 to 6 days a week with a trainer over FaceTime. He uses a hammock-and-palm-tree outdoor gym with a small barbell set and dumbbells, and does ring pull-up and knee-raise combos and a barbell shoulder press and crunch combo. His cheat meals are pasta, bread, pancakes and waffles. Dr. Mike's main points are that Kravitz has had abs since his 20s, so the diet didn't create them, and that genetics matter. He also says store-bought produce is nutritionally about equal to home-grown, that herbs add flavor with few calories, and that Kravitz must be eating more protein than the clip shows. He adds that a raw vegan diet is one option but not a requirement, and that regular exercise matters more as you age.

For you, Dr. Mike boils it down to three rules. (1) Eat about 1 g of protein per pound of body weight per day, spread over 3 to 5 meals. (2) Eat enough calories to hold your weight, or a deficit if you need to lose fat. (3) Make most of your food healthy: lean meats or lean vegan products, vegetables, fruit, whole grains and healthy fats. The rest of his advice covers activity and recovery:
- Get about 10,000 steps a day.
- Do at least two resistance sessions a week of 20 minutes or more. Use compound lifts like rows, deadlifts, pull-ups and pulldowns, with sets of 5 to 20 reps taken close to failure.
- Optionally add mobility work, such as deep stretching, yoga or full-range lifting.
- Sleep well, keep stress manageable and keep up your friendships.
- Cheat meals are fine once or twice a week if your weight is stable, or less often if you don't want to overdo it.
- You don't need a fancy gym. A barbell and dumbbells are enough.
- Combo movements, like pull-up with knee raise, save time.
- Avoid over-arching your back on overhead presses.
