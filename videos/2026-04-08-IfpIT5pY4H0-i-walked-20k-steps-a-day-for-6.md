## Summary

A creator describes a 60-day experiment in which he walked about 20,000 steps per day while eating to appetite on “clean” foods and claims he lost 8 lbs of fat, reached near single-digit body fat, felt more energetic, and did not feel hungry. He frames this against his prior history: a bulk from about 175 lbs, a cut to 145/144 lbs, a rebound to 157 lbs, and a reference to YouTube lifter Alex Leonitis’s claim that natural lifters often underestimate body fat. The walking protocol was at least 10 miles per day on a walking pad at about 2 mph and 8% incline, which Google estimated as 20,000–28,000 steps; he also uses the ACSM walking equation and METs at a 153-lb body weight to estimate roughly 1,700 gross calories burned per day, conservatively counting about 1,000 extra calories burned daily. He notes that 1,000 extra calories per day should theoretically produce about 2 lbs lost per week, but he lost about 1 lb per week, implying he was eating roughly 500 extra calories per day, or about a Big Mac’s worth, while still losing fat. He also claims the low-intensity incline walking fell into zone 2 cardio, where after 10–30 minutes fat may supply up to about 70% of energy, helping him lose fat while preserving strength and muscle during sandbag and calisthenics work.

Actionable takeaways: if the user wants fat loss without severe hunger, consider adding low-intensity walking during sedentary activities such as computer work, phone use, or leisure time, rather than relying only on crash diets or intense exercise. Practical steps include starting with a realistic walking pad or treadmill routine, tracking steps, time, speed, incline, weekly weight, waist size, energy, hunger, and strength, and using an ACSM/METS calculator or wearable to estimate energy expenditure. The user should decide whether a slow, high-volume walking approach fits their schedule, joints, and health status, and should consult a clinician if they have heart, blood pressure, orthopedic, metabolic, or other medical concerns. Useful research leads include NEAT, zone 2 cardio, fat oxidation during prolonged low-intensity exercise, incline walking, walking-pad adherence, and individual variability in calorie burn and appetite response. Follow-up questions to consider: How many steps or walking hours are realistically sustainable? Is 8% incline safe for the user’s knees, hips, back, or cardiovascular system? Are calories being underestimated or overestimated? Is weight loss plateauing because food intake increased more than expected? And is the user preserving muscle, sleep quality, and training performance while increasing daily movement?
