## Summary

Peter Zeihan argues that deglobalization has passed the point of no return because the US never embraced globalization for economic reasons. After World War II it was a security bargain: the US used its Navy to guarantee open seas and access to its market, and in return allies let it write their security policy, which helped win the Cold War. That rationale has faded, for three reasons. First, the world is now three to four times the size of the US economy, so the implicit subsidy is less tenable, and an overextended China depends on globalization while leaning on Russia, its main raw-materials supplier. Second, US politics has realigned. Unions have left the Democratic coalition and are being courted by both sides, and the business and national-security conservatives who favored globalization have been pushed out of the Republican coalition. The Biden administration has kept or expanded Trump's tariffs, with the Aerospace deal with Europe the only exception. Third, demographics: urbanization has cut birth rates, so the world is running out of the under-40 consumers that trade relies on. China, Russia, Germany, Korea, Indonesia, India and Brazil are all aging fast, and by about 2040–2045 the average American will be younger than the average Brazilian. He says a return to globalization would take a security scare that frightens the US more than other countries, and the Ukraine war isn't one. Even a pro-globalization president would need roughly six years to unwind the anti-trade policy now in place, which he thinks is long enough for the system to be gone.

For you, the video is an argument for planning around a world with less global trade, not a task list. If you accept his thesis, the practical points are to treat supply chains that depend on China or other far-off suppliers as a risk, and to favor domestic or nearby sourcing (the US and its neighbors) over long-distance imports. Also, expect a lasting trend of tariffs and protectionism whichever party wins, and expect the aging of major economies to reduce consumer demand abroad. Treat his timelines (a roughly six-year window, and China "gone") as one analyst's forecast, not settled fact. The video is also dated: it treats Biden as the sitting president and predates what has happened since. Before making financial or business decisions, check his claims against current trade and demographic data and other analysts' views.
