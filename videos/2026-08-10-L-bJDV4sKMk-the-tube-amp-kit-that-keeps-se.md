## Summary

The video walks through building StewMac's VF18 tube amp kit, which has an exposed-tube design so the preamp tubes can be swapped easily. The builder is on their second tube amp build, after a '57 mini tweed clone. The build starts with mounting the tube sockets, switches, transformers and lamp and fuse holders. A first power-up test follows, where the fuse reads 220 V instead of the expected 120 V and the builder doesn't resolve it. Next come the turret board, with pushback wire, resistors and capacitors, and then the pots and input jacks. The final voltage checks come after the tubes go in. The builder gives practical tips along the way:
- Measure each resistor before installing it.
- Build on a bench rather than inside the chassis.
- Use heat shrink on switch lugs.
- Remove bulbs, fuses and chips before soldering their sockets.
- Heat the lug and feed solder from the opposite side, with the iron around 360°C.
- Clip alligator-clip heat sinks on capacitor leads.

The safety points are that the large filter capacitor stays dangerous after power-off and must be drained with a "snuffer stick," that the amp must never run without a speaker cabinet, and that it needs a speaker cable rather than an instrument cable. The final test is two guitarists at Donley's Music playing it through the builder's own speaker cabinet. They praise its bass response, its clean and country tones, and its versatility. Swapping to a hotter preamp tube gives a much more driven, fuzz-like sound, though the clean setting barely breaks up even at max volume. The builder concludes it earned a spot in their rig.

If you're considering the kit, the video suggests it is doable for someone with basic soldering skills and patience. It is not a first-ever project, so practice soldering first and read the full instructions. Budget for tools: a multimeter (for resistor checks and DC voltage readings), needle-nose pliers, a soldering iron near 360°C, heat shrink, alligator clips, a helping-hands stand, and a snuffer stick. You'll also need a speaker cabinet matched to the amp's impedance setting (4 ohms in the video) and a proper speaker cable. Follow the safety routine every time: drain the capacitor before touching anything, keep your hands off live circuits, let tubes cool before swapping them, and rest the head on wooden blocks so it doesn't sit on the glass tubes. Compare your voltage readings against the instruction schematic (the builder's were within about 2 V once tubes were installed). Also note that the 220 V reading the builder saw at the fuse was never explained, so if you see something similar, investigate it before powering up. To change the amp's character, buy a few extra preamp tubes, such as the 12AY7 for clean tones and a hotter option for crunch. Keep in mind that the video is affiliate-supported and includes no price, so check StewMac's current cost and stock before committing.
