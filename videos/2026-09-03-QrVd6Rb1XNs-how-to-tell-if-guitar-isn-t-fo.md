## Summary

The creator, a guitar YouTuber, expands on his earlier short "How to Tell if Guitar Isn't for You" after heavy backlash, and he reads through critical and supportive comments. The short's four signs were: you can't name many famous guitar players, you don't consume guitar content online, you rarely think about guitar, and you hate practicing. He defends the first point with a story from his jazz-school audition. The guitar professor asked him to name famous jazz guitarists, he couldn't, and he now sees that as a fair test of his interest. He says the target was serious aspiring players, and he concedes that favorite bands or songs can count as inspiration. He rejects the "gatekeeping" charge and says he doesn't care about hate comments. On practice, he says motivation is unreliable and discipline is what lasts. He says heavy dopamine sources like social media, gaming, weed, vaping and porn can make slow guitar progress feel boring. He also says skills decay if you stop playing, so someone who isn't committed may waste money and time. He pushes back on commenters who call this discouraging or say guitar is for everyone, and on one who said four hours a day doesn't help beginners. He ends by pitching the waitlist for his "52-Week Guitar Player" course.

For you, the useful part is a self-check rather than a verdict on whether to quit. Can you point to bands, songs or players that make you want to play? Do you voluntarily seek out guitar content, and does guitar come to mind between sessions? If practice feels like a chore most of the time, start with easy songs such as Smoke on the Water, Knocking on Heaven's Door or Sunshine of Your Love. He says a Sweet Child o' Mine riff is realistic within about four months, while Johnny B. Goode or Hey Joe may not be. Cut back on instant-gratification habits that make slow progress feel dull. Plan on consistent practice, because skill fades without it. Treat his claims as opinion, not evidence. He offers no data, and several commenters note you can enjoy guitar as a casual hobby without knowing famous players. Whether that fits you depends on whether you want to get good or just have fun.
