## Summary

Dr. Mao, a 38th generation Chinese medicine doctor, shared findings from studying 100+ Chinese centinarians over 20 years. The centinarians shared consistent habits: they ate until 75% full (not overeating), maintained emotional equanimity and forgiveness, walked 1-2 hours daily throughout their lives, slept eight hours consistently, and lived in tight-knit rural communities. Central to his philosophy is that 80% of chronic disease originates in the mind—stress triggers cortisol and adrenaline that create inflammation and accelerate aging. Different emotions affect different organs (anger impacts the liver, worry damages digestion). He contrasted this with modern Western culture's extremes: overeating due to food abundance, overtraining, extreme cold plunges, and chronic stress—patterns absent in the long-lived populations he studied.

To apply this: eat until comfortably full rather than stuffed; prioritize stress management and emotional peace as the foundation of health; maintain consistent daily movement (walking 1-2 hours) rather than extreme workouts; sleep eight hours with an early bedtime; build genuine community connections even in cities; and practice moderation in all activities. Practically, prepare for environmental hazards (fireproof your home if fire-prone, use air filters, add indoor plants to reduce indoor pollution, maintain emergency kits). Recognize that most worries (98%) never materialize—redirect that mental energy toward controllable factors: diet, activity, sleep, and emotional regulation.
