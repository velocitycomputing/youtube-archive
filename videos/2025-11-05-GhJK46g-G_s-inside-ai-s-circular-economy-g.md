## Summary

Circular AI infrastructure deals were presented as a financial-engineering web centered on Nvidia: Nvidia reportedly plans up to $100 billion in OpenAI, OpenAI has a $300 billion Oracle cloud deal, Nvidia buys $6.3 billion of CoreWeave services while owning about 7% of CoreWeave, OpenAI may pay CoreWeave up to $22.4 billion, AMD gives OpenAI 10% equity for chip commitments, Nvidia invests $5 billion in Intel, and the U.S. takes a 15% cut of Nvidia and AMD China chip sales. It compares this to 2008 synthetic CDOs, notes GPU rental rates have fallen 75% in some markets, and details export-control arbitrage via Nebius in Amsterdam, Nvidia’s $700 million investment, Microsoft’s $17.4 billion Nebius agreement, G42 in the UAE, and tiered chip access: unlimited for allies, 50,000 H100s for Tier 2, and downgraded chips for China, Russia, North Korea, and Iran. It also cites weak AI economics: 20% of U.S. adults use ChatGPT, enterprise AI ROI averages 6%, 80% report no significant bottom-line impact, 95% of GenAI pilots fail to reach production, and GitHub Copilot productivity gains are small.

Actionable takeaways: if investing or working in AI infrastructure, treat vendor financing, related-party sales, SPV leases, and circular customer-supplier deals as red flags; ask whether revenue is recurring, cash-backed, and independent of Nvidia, AMD, OpenAI, Oracle, CoreWeave, Nebius, Microsoft, and xAI cross-investments, and check filings for off-balance-sheet debt, lease obligations, customer concentration, export-control exposure, and GPU depreciation assumptions. Track concrete indicators: GPU rental prices, data-center utilization, enterprise AI ROI, pilot-to-production rates, capex versus operating cash flow, and regulatory changes to U.S. export tiers; read the Bloomberg circular-deals report and review SEC filings for Nvidia, OpenAI, Oracle, CoreWeave, AMD, Intel, xAI, Nebius, Microsoft, and G42.
