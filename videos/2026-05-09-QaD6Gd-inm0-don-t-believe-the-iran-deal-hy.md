## Summary

Ellie Piaps of the Israel Defense and Security Forum argued that reports of a near U.S.-Iran deal are overstated and that President Trump’s broader aim is regime change, not a premature off-ramp. She framed Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s May 5 statement that “Operation Epic Fury” had ended as a legal and political move tied to the 60-day War Powers timeline, noting the announcement came 66 days after the operation began and could reset a new clock for a separate Strait of Hormuz mission. The discussion covered earlier U.S. diplomacy under Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, and Marco Rubio, the failed June “Operation Midnight Hammer” approach, Iran’s refusal to discuss its nuclear program, U.S. red lines on nuclear capability, and the possibility that continued economic blockade and sanctions—not immediate strikes—could pressure the Iranian regime. Piaps also cited Trump’s posture toward the UAE attack, Gulf states’ concern about Iran, and the idea that Trump is not constrained by antiwar voices such as Tucker Carlson or Candace Owens, by $4–$5 gas prices, or by midterm fears; she suggested Democratic control of Congress could even benefit Trump politically. She concluded that the war’s long-term significance depends on whether it ends in regime change or a perceived stalemate, with implications for Israel, the Gulf, China, Taiwan, and future U.S. willingness to project power.

No direct health implications are present; the actionable value is geopolitical and economic. The user should treat deal rumors cautiously, distinguish official statements from anonymous leaks, and watch whether Iran’s response addresses the nuclear program, whether the U.S. maintains the Strait of Hormuz blockade and sanctions, and whether Israel retains freedom of action against Iranian targets.
