## Summary

Chinese state oil companies—PetroChina, Sinopec, CNOOC, and Zhenhua Oil—reportedly paused seaborne Russian crude imports after the Trump administration imposed sweeping sanctions on Rosneft, Lukoil, intermediaries, and Russia’s shadow fleet, threatening secondary sanctions and exclusion from dollar-linked finance. The video frames this as a major blow to Putin’s war economy because oil and gas make up about a quarter of Russia’s federal budget, while China and India together account for roughly 80% of Russia’s crude exports; China has been importing more than 2 million barrels per day by sea and pipeline, and India, especially Reliance Industries, has been the largest buyer of seaborne Russian crude. It also claims oil prices jumped more than 5%, Reliance is recalibrating purchases despite a reported 10-year deal for about 0.5 million barrels per day, Chinese “teapot” refiners are afraid to fill the gap, and Russia’s central bank cut its key rate to 16.5%, the fourth cut since June, signaling stagnation and inflation not reaching target until 2027.

Actionable: monitor whether the Chinese pause is temporary or becomes a permanent reduction, whether Indian refiners keep cutting Russian barrels, and whether secondary sanctions actually freeze assets or mainly create compliance fear. Track Russian Urals discounts, seaborne export volumes, shadow-fleet enforcement, OPEC+ spare capacity, and fuel-price pass-through to consumers; if you have energy or investment exposure, review exposure to crude-price spikes, ruble weakness, and inflation. Useful follow-up questions: will Beijing permanently scale back, can small Chinese refiners absorb the barrels, can Russia reroute to other buyers, and will lost oil revenue affect Russian social spending, elite cohesion, or Ukraine negotiations.
