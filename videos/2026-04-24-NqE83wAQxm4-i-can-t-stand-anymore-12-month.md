## Summary

A year after the speaker last stood, the update frames long COVID/ME as a crisis of testimonial credibility, invoking Wittgenstein’s link between pain, suffering, and expression to argue that medicine often treats patient experience as inaccessible. The speaker describes severe orthostatic intolerance after COVID about 18 months earlier, a month-long near-fatal hospitalization a year ago, and current limits: working from bed one to two hours daily, tolerating only about 15–30 minutes in a wheelchair (with a three-hour best), and violent suffocation/chest pain with even slight verticalization. They discuss uncertainty about microvascular damage, a specialist’s 5% chance of benefit from a medication they cannot tolerate at effective doses, inability to travel for testing despite remote consultations with top UK, US, and German long-COVID/microvascular specialists, and the burden on their partner, who has had long COVID for over three years and works from home for the BBC. The update also touches on acceptance through low-demand pleasures, faking normalcy, Christopher Hitchens, political hope, and the economic argument that a few billion in long-COVID/ME investment could be rational because lost economic participation exceeds that cost.

Actionable takeaways are to treat suspected long-COVID/ME orthostatic intolerance as a serious medical problem rather than simple deconditioning: track verticalization tolerance, heart rate/blood pressure/oxygen symptoms, and post-exertional crashes; ask a clinician about autonomic/microvascular evaluation, home-based testing, medication options and dose-titration limits, and whether small-risk treatments are worth trying when percentages are low; and consider telehealth or home visits if travel triggers severe symptoms. For caregivers and patients, build a medical decision system—partner transcription, symptom diary, medication list, consent-risk log—because complex options are hard to manage while ill. Follow-up questions include: What is causing the verticalization intolerance, such as POTS, hypovolemia, mast-cell activation, cardiac, pulmonary, or microvascular dysfunction? Can safe micro-dosing of therapy be designed? What emergency plan exists if standing causes suffocation? What disability, workplace, and household supports are needed? And for policy-minded viewers, research long-COVID/ME funding, diagnostic access, and economic modeling of lost productivity versus treatment investment.
