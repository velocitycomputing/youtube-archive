## Summary

Matteo Mancuso walks through five escalating layers of improvising over a C major backing track (C to E7 to F minor movement). Level 1 is the minor pentatonic: don't wait to know everything, learn all five boxes, mix 2- and 3-notes-per-string shapes, and repeat each pattern across three octaves (anchored on the C notes) so you move diagonally instead of up and down one box. Level 2 adds the major scale's remaining notes as landing and target notes, mixed with pentatonic. He stresses interval vocabulary (especially thirds and sixths, plus string skipping and Eric Johnson-style open-voiced triads) and thinking in note functions relative to the key rather than note names. Level 3 uses arpeggios from the harmonized major scale (C, Dm, Em, F, Am, Cmaj7, Fmaj7), with an up-in-C, down-in-Dm, up-in-Em exercise to visualize them in one position. Level 4 handles the outside chords: visualize the E7 in the closest position, use A harmonic minor over it and F melodic minor over the Fm (a major scale with a minor third), and let your line dictate the chords, not the reverse. Level 5 is chromaticism, using the bebop major scale (major scale with a minor sixth). Chromatic notes should be passing notes on weak beats, and you should land on scale tones. A "wrong" note is fixed by resolving it. He then plays a full solo combining everything, and says he practices about 4 hours a day (it was 12 as a teenager).

For you, the practical plan is to work through the levels one at a time over a backing track (the track is on Mancuso's Patreon, but any C major track with an E7 and Fm would do). First, learn the five pentatonic boxes and the three-octave C anchor points, then phrase within one box with bends and slides before moving on. Second, drill the major scale in mixed 3-and-2-notes-per-string positions and practice thirds and sixths with string skipping, naming notes by function (3rd, #4) instead of letter. Third, run the Cmaj scale arpeggio exercise (up C, down Dm, up Em, down F, and so on) in a single neck position. Fourth, for the E7 and Fm bars, find the nearest voicing to your current position, then practice A harmonic minor and F melodic minor in that same area, and try turning a major-scale phrase minor by flattening the third. Fifth, add chromatic approach notes only as passing tones on weak beats, resolving to a chord or scale tone. Throughout, listen to players who use these devices and copy their vocabulary, and don't let note names or gear-knowledge gaps stop you from improvising early.
