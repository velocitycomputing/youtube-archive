## Summary

Retirement planner Dan Hayler argues that retiring at 60 is financially viable for many people, despite the "bridge"—seven years funding retirement entirely from savings before state pension arrives at 67. He presents two case studies: Neil and Sarah (£600k saved, £35k annual spending) can retire today but fear pulling the trigger; Mark (£350k, made redundant, needs £25k/year) requires modest supplementary income. Hayler details five tax strategies unique to the bridge years—pension income smoothing, phasing tax-free cash draws, moving investments into ISAs, buying missing national insurance years, and harvesting losses—worth £15–30k over seven years. He emphasizes the bucket strategy (2 years cash, 5 years all-weather funds, rest in equities) to psychologically manage watching your pot decline, and warns that the real risk isn't running out of money but running out of healthy years waiting for 67.

To act on this, model your specific bridge-years drawdown to confirm you can reach 67 with adequate remaining capital. Check pension access rules (minimum age 57 post-April 2028) and verify you'll hit 35 qualifying years for full state pension—buy missing years at £824 each if short. Implement the tax playbook: draw pension income to fill your empty personal allowance (£12,570), phase tax-free cash across seven years, and move taxable investments into ISAs. Consider light part-time work (£5–10k annually) in early retirement to reduce portfolio drawdown. Before resigning, answer four non-financial questions: what gives you purpose, daily structure, social connection, and health focus—the financial plan keeps lights on, but the life plan keeps you alive.
