## Summary

Medieval bread was presented as a nutrient-dense staple: a 1324 York-area peasant allegedly ate 2–3 lbs of bread daily, supplying 70% of calories, while modern claims include one in three adults pre-diabetic, celiac disease up 400% since the 1950s, white bread glycemic index 75 versus table sugar 65, and supermarket loaves needing 20–30 ingredients. The transcript names Norman Borlaug’s semi-dwarf “miracle wheat,” chromosome shifts from einkorn 14 to emmer 28 to modern bread wheat 42, University of Kansas findings of stronger immune responses to modern wheat, and University of Florence/Italian studies where einkorn or ancient-grain sourdough improved IBS symptoms in 80% of participants and produced lower glucose/insulin responses. It also discusses stone grinding versus roller milling, loss of bran/germ nutrients, long sourdough fermentation reducing phytates/lectins, commercial yeast and short production times, additives such as HFCS, soybean oil, mono/diglycerides, calcium propionate, azodicarbonamide, chlorine/benzoyl peroxide, and glyphosate pre-harvest desiccation, claiming >70% of non-organic wheat products tested contain glyphosate and WHO classifies it as a probable carcinogen.

Actionable steps: if bread causes bloating, fatigue, brain fog, or blood-sugar crashes, reduce or eliminate supermarket enriched white bread and track symptoms for 2–4 weeks; try 100% heritage-grain (einkorn, emmer, spelt) stone-ground, naturally fermented sourdough with 12–24+ hour fermentation, or make it at home with a starter; choose organic or verified low-residue sources if concerned about glyphosate; consider replacing bread with properly prepared oats, barley, quinoa, or rice. Before going gluten-free, consult a clinician if celiac disease is possible, because testing usually requires ongoing gluten intake; ask about celiac serology, glucose/A1c, IBS evaluation, and whether fermentation or ancient grains help. Useful research leads: randomized trials comparing modern wheat, ancient wheat, and sourdough fermentation; glyphosate/microbiome studies; glycemic response of stone-ground vs roller-milled flour; and individualized elimination/reintroduction protocols.
