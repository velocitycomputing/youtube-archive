## Summary

The transcript centers on omega-3 fatty acids and biological aging, using epigenetic clocks—especially DNA GrimAge and DNA PhenoAge, developed from work by Dr. Steve Horvath at UCLA—to estimate biological age from DNA methylation patterns. It cites a 2019 prospective study linking higher omega-3 intake to slower age acceleration, and a 2024 study suggesting dose thresholds: about 1 gram/day for women and about 461 mg/day for men. A Swiss randomized controlled trial in healthy, mostly already-active adults found that 1 gram/day of omega-3 slowed epigenetic aging, while vitamin D alone at 2,000 IU/day did not, and added resistance training—30 minutes, 3 days/week—had limited effect because participants were already active. Omega-3 plus vitamin D slowed aging by about 3.3 months; omega-3 plus vitamin D plus resistance training slowed it by about 3.8 months, reduced pre-frailty by 40%, and reduced invasive cancer by 61%; omega-3 alone reduced infections and falls by 13%. The speaker links these effects to inflammation, citing a Japanese centenarian study where suppressed inflammation predicted longevity and better cognition, and mentions mechanisms involving EPA, leukotrienes, prostaglandins, specialized pro-resolving mediators, and autophagy.

Actionable takeaways: consider whether 1 gram/day of omega-3 is appropriate, especially if intake is low, but check with a clinician if you take blood thinners, have bleeding risk, upcoming surgery, or fish allergies; ask about testing omega-3 index, vitamin D level, and inflammatory markers like hsCRP before and after intervention. If vitamin D is deficient, 2,000 IU/day may be insufficient, so discuss a higher dose—around 4,000 IU/day was mentioned as often needed to correct deficiency—based on blood levels. Maintain or add resistance training, but recognize that in already-active people the added aging benefit may be smaller. Useful follow-up questions: What is my current omega-3 intake and omega-3 index? Am I vitamin D deficient? Which EPA/DHA product and dose fit my goals and medications? Could a baseline epigenetic clock test be meaningful, or is it too noisy/expensive for personal use? What is my inflammation burden, and are there other drivers—sleep, smoking, obesity, poor diet, pollution—that need addressing alongside supplementation?
