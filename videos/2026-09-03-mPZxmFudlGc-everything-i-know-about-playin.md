## Summary

Brandon Deon, who runs the 52-Week Guitar Player online school, lays out his approach to playing fast. He says two schools of thought both matter. The "slow" method starts at a manageable tempo and raises it gradually. The "fast" method has you play at a tempo that's too fast and clean it up over several sessions. His recommendation is to find your limit first. A simple test is to play 16th notes on a 1-2-3-4 exercise for one minute and see where you can barely keep up. Then use the fast method to jump about 30 BPM above that, for example 100 to 130. Repeat the cycle once you plateau, which in effect applies the slow method over the long run. He covers four routes to sounding fast: alternate picking, legato (hammer-ons and pull-offs, picking only the first and last note of a group), sweep picking, and tapping. His main technical points concern picking mechanics. With two notes per string, start on an upstroke when ascending and a downstroke when descending, so you're always "inside picking" toward the next string. Downward pick slanting suits even groups of notes per string, and odd groups need an upward slant. He sometimes replaces alternate picking with a sweep when a line is hard. For stamina, he suggests playing continuously for 1 to 3 minutes at a tempo slightly below your limit. The video also promotes his paid program (enrollment was open until September 30 or until 100 students signed up, with a waiting list otherwise).

For practice, test your 16th-note limit on one simple exercise, then practice at a tempo well above it until it cleans up. Raise the tempo and repeat once you plateau, and log your tempos, since he says untracked practice won't improve. Do a daily alternate-picking exercise, such as a single-string run, three-notes-per-string scales, or a mid-length pattern. Pick the stroke direction on each string deliberately: upstroke first going up, downstroke first going down, and sweep awkward passages. Add a few minutes of legato work, such as three-note-per-string scales with a pick on only the first note of each string. Finish with a 1 to 3 minute continuous run at a slightly slower tempo to build stamina. He says consistency is the main reason people don't get faster. You don't need to buy anything, because the exercises are free and the course is only a sales pitch.
