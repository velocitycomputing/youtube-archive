## Summary

Maggie Haberman, White House correspondent at The New York Times and co-author with Jonathan Swan of *Regime Change* (a book on the first 14 months of Trump's second term), tells Ezra Klein that "running out of steam" is the wrong frame: Trump is "running into Trump reality." Some policies persist, including DEI and trans-athlete orders, border enforcement, and unilateral government-by-fiat moves like shutting down USAID. Meanwhile the trade war, the DOJ overhaul and the Iran war have produced chaos and little strategy. On Iran, she says the war grew out of Trump's long hawkishness, his sense of being under personal threat (the Iran-linked murder-for-hire plot, the Butler shooting, the hacked Susie Wiles emails) and a Feb. 11 Situation Room visit from Netanyahu. Trump's own CIA director and Secretary of State thought the regime-change part was farcical, but Trump judged the war worth trying anyway, and the Venezuela/Maduro operation had convinced him such moves work. She and Klein agree there is "no Trumpism, only Trump." Haberman adds that MAGA is a fracturing coalition: the Epstein files fight was the first big break with his base, though challengers like Massie and Greene lost and his primary power is intact. She describes an information diet built on Truth Social, aide Natalie Harp's printouts of positive stories, and friendly Fox coverage, inside a small, informal circle with no real interagency process. She also points to unprecedented self-dealing and corruption that Republicans in Congress ignore, and says Trump believes things always work out for him and isn't much worried about impeachment or legal exposure. The transcript cuts off mid-answer on why scandals slide off him.
