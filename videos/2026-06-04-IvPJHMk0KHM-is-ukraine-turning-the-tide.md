## Summary

Max Bergmann and Maria Snegovaya, based on a recent Kyiv trip, argued that Ukraine’s position has improved despite intensified Russian attacks: Ukrainian drone innovation has shifted momentum, enabling deep strikes on Russian oil facilities, ships, refineries, logistics, and the St. Petersburg Economic Forum backdrop, while Russia faces airport closures, gasoline shortages and rationing in Crimea and St. Petersburg, inflation, sanctions pressure, mobile-internet restrictions hurting small businesses, and a reported 8–10 point decline in Putin’s approval since December 2025 in VTsIOM and other polls. They cited roughly 1.5 million Russian casualties and possibly 500,000 dead, framed the war as longer than WWII’s Eastern Front, and pointed to Ukraine’s defense industry, the EU’s €90 billion lifeline, Starlink denial to Russian forces, US-made Hornet strike UAVs, and Gulf/Middle East drone partnerships as reducing dependence on Washington; at the same time, Ukraine faces conscription strain, air-defense shortages, and record Russian ballistic-missile/drone strikes on Kyiv and energy infrastructure, with Zelenskyy expected to seek Patriot interceptors, sanctions on Russia’s shadow fleet, and capital to scale drone interceptors at the G7 in Evian.

Actionable leads: monitor whether Patriot interceptors and air-defense production arrive before winter, because energy-grid attacks create concrete public-health and humanitarian risks—loss of heating, medical care, water, and mental-health strain—plus track Russian economic indicators (fuel shortages, inflation, airport disruptions, VTsIOM methodology changes) and Ukrainian conscription/adaptation as early signals of morale. For research or decisions, compare Ukraine’s drone-export diplomacy with Russia’s Global South arms sales, follow EU €90 billion implementation, Gulf defense partnerships, sanctions enforcement against shadow-fleet oil, and whether pro-Kremlin debate shifts from victory claims to nuclear rhetoric or negotiation; useful follow-up questions are whether Ukraine can sustain deep strikes without US aid, whether Russia’s defense industry scales faster than Ukrainian innovation, and whether a genuine Russian offer to drop maximalist Donbas demands would change Ukrainian willingness to negotiate rather than seek a durable stalemate.
