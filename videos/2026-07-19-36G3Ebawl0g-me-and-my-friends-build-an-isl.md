## Summary

Oliver and his girlfriend Helena, along with friends including Kasha from Poland, continued building a log cabin on their island in Finnish wilderness after Oliver's six-month mandatory military service. Over three days, they worked on stacking logs for the cabin walls, designing and cutting a large window opening, troubleshooting their broken electric trolling engine, caring for their dog Seni (who refuses to swim despite attempts with treats), encountering a black snake while swimming, attempting to fish from the dock, and measuring and cataloguing all remaining logs to plan the final construction layers.

If replicating this project, key takeaways are: measure, mark, and inventory all logs before construction rather than working ad-hoc—Oliver acknowledged this mistake; position difficult cuts (windows, doors) on the bottom of logs rather than the top, since chainsaw work at height is significantly harder and slower; bring friends to distribute physical labor and maintain morale on grueling days; overstock consumables like marking pencils and tools; and use AI or planning software to optimize log stacking patterns before placement. The project shows that even without prior building experience, methodical upfront planning and community support make ambitious log construction achievable.
