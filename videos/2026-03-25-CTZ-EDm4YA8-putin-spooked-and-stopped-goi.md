## Summary

Julia Ioffe’s Puck piece, citing TV Rain and Mikhail Fishman, reported unverified “chatter” that Vladimir Putin was deeply unsettled by the US-Israel war against Iran and by reports that Israel used Tehran traffic cameras to target the Ayatollah, prompting rumors that he stopped going to the Kremlin, cleared public events, and ordered Moscow-wide mobile internet and VPN disruptions in a city of 13 million people; the official explanation was Ukrainian drone protection, but commentators including Ioffe, Fishman, and pro-Kremlin outlets like Tsargrad suggested personal-security motives, while Mark Galeotti was more skeptical, and the discussion also referenced Muammar Gaddafi’s death, a rumor that Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was in Moscow for medical treatment, Alexander Prokhanov’s “immortality” remark, and psychological readings of Putin’s grandiosity, sociopathy, narcissism, and fear of humiliation.

Actionable takeaways: treat the Kremlin-absence, Khamenei-in-Moscow, and paranoia claims as unverified and cross-check them with independent telecom monitors such as NetBlocks or OONI, Russian-language OSINT, and the named sources; if traveling or operating in Moscow, prepare for communications blackouts with offline maps, backup connectivity, emergency contacts, and medical contingency plans because outages can affect emergency response and increase anxiety; for research, separate the personal-security/paranoia hypothesis from the drone-defense hypothesis and ask whether shutdowns correlate with specific events, whether the traffic-camera hacking claim has technical evidence, and whether Putin’s strategic benefit from US/Iran disorder outweighs any fear response.
