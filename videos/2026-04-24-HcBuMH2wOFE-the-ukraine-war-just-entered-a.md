## Summary

Michael Kofman, a Carnegie Endowment senior fellow and War on the Rocks/Russia Contingency host, framed the Russia-Ukraine war in its fifth year as positional, attritional, and increasingly an exhaustion campaign against energy, refineries, export infrastructure, and defense-industrial production. He described recurring seasonal patterns—fronts stabilizing in winter, Russian offensive activity intensifying from March through December, and 3-to-4-month adaptation cycles—while arguing that discrete seasonal offensives have largely been replaced by continuous pressure across a 1,200-km front, with priority sectors in Donetsk and secondary pressure in Zaporizhzhia/Dnipropetrovsk where weaker Ukrainian territorial-defense and 150-series brigades faced flatter terrain. He detailed Russian tactical evolution from 2024 mechanized assault with small infantry detachments to 2025 infiltration and light motorized assault using motorcycles, ATVs, and dune buggies, accepting high losses to cross drone kill zones; he said Russian armor losses have declined, armored vehicles are roughly at or above prewar numbers, but the overall force is about 50% larger and ground forces more than doubled, making equipment less the constraint than manpower, air defense, and drone superiority. He named contested places including Lyman, Kupiansk, Dobropillia, Vuhledar, Pokrovsk, Sloviansk, Kramatorsk, Belgorod, and Sumy, and discussed Ukraine’s air-defense-strike campaign against Tor M2, Pantsir, Buk systems, tactical radars, and interceptor-drone networks, with the aim of creating corridors for winged ISR and mid-range strikes 50 km or more behind the front, where Russian forces are less dispersed than in the 20–30 km forward zone. He also covered Ukrainian counterattacks in Kupiansk and Zaporizhzhia by assault regiments and air-assault brigades—naming Skala 425/225, the 1st Assault Regiment, Code 92/475, Code 9.2, Hartia, and the 13th National Guard Brigade—arguing that drone-enabled, unit-specific methods can retake gray-zone terrain without Russian-style losses, while warning that cities like Kramatorsk and Sloviansk could suffer Pokrovsk-like degradation if Russian fiber-optic FPV drones establish fire control.
