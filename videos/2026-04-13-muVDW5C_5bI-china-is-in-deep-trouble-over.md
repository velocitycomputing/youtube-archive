## Summary

President Trump’s blockade of Iran is framed as a direct strategic shock to China because China’s industrial economy depends on Gulf energy through the Strait of Hormuz: the cited report from Adam Craft and his team at the Corgi Group/Collections Operations Resource Group for Intelligence, discussed by Ryan McBeth, claims China imports about 5 million barrels per day of crude through Hormuz and about 30% of its LNG imports, with 2025 crude imports around 11.55 million barrels per day and 40–50% of those imports coming via Hormuz, while the Middle East supplies more than half of China’s oil mix. The report says China’s strategic and commercial petroleum reserves total roughly 1.1–1.3 billion barrels, giving about a 100-day buffer under normal conditions, but the disruption clock began February 28, 43 days had elapsed, and the remaining window is about 57 days to June 8, 2026. For the user, the actionable move is to treat June 8 as a monitoring trigger and track whether the Hormuz disruption persists, whether China releases strategic reserves, whether Russian overland pipeline capacity can actually expand, and whether Chinese refineries can keep producing diesel, jet fuel, and naval fuel rather than merely storing crude.
