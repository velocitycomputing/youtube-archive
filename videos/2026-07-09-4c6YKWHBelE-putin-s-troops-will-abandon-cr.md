## Summary

Lieutenant General Ben Hodges, former US Army Europe commander, argued that Ukraine has shifted the war’s momentum by developing long-range precision drone strikes—over 1,000 km—that bypass Russian air defenses and hit oil and gas infrastructure, including targets around the Kerch Strait, refineries, logistics hubs, S-400 radar sites, and Pantsir systems. He claimed these attacks have helped isolate Crimea, damaged bridges and facilities, disrupted military logistics in Zaporizhzhia and Crimea, and contributed to fuel shortages, queues reportedly lasting over an hour, and a Russian ban on fuel sales in occupied territory. Hodges also discussed Putin’s possible information isolation, the psychological impact on Russians, Lukashenka’s apparent distancing from Russia after Zelenskyy’s pressure, Trump’s possible reassessment after meeting NATO chief Mark Rutte, and Zelenskyy’s announced 40-day offensive as partly an information campaign aimed at changing the narrative that Russia must win. He said Ukraine’s likely strategy is continued isolation of Crimea—potentially leading to Russian withdrawal rather than an amphibious landing—plus sustained attacks on oil exports to China and India to undermine Russia’s war economy, while warning that Western NATO war games, including Germany’s simulation of a Russian attack, often omit Russia’s “gray zone” operations: airport drone incursions, sabotage of cables and pipelines, assassinations such as the Skripal poisoning in Salisbury, and shadow-fleet activity designed to pressure Western publics.
