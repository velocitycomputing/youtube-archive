## Summary

George Barros explains that Ukraine has achieved drone supremacy in tactical areas and is systematically isolating Russian forces through "battlefield air interdiction"—destroying trucks and trains to deny Russian supplies and freedom of movement, particularly to Crimea. Ukraine is also reintroducing mechanized vehicles and combined arms tactics after years of positional warfare, developing indigenous long-range weapons like the Flamingo cruise missile and glide bombs, and striking Russia's defense industrial base. Meanwhile, Russia's operational tempo has increased despite these strikes, and diplomatic efforts have resumed with potential Trump administration involvement, though Putin remains intransigent and continues frontal assault tactics with mounting casualties.

The actionable takeaway is that the West should urgently provide mature systems—particularly Tomahawk cruise missiles—to enable Ukraine to strike critical Russian industrial nodes like the Alabuga Special Economic Zone (which assembles Iranian-supplied drones) and Uralvagonzavod (Russia's primary tank factory). This time-bounded window of Ukrainian advantage will close as Russia develops countermeasures, so immediate additional military support is essential both to halt Russian gains and to strengthen Ukraine's negotiating position in any future talks.
