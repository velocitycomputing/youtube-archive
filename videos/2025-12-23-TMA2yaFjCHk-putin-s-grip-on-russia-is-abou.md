## Summary

Former U.S. Ambassador Kurt Volker, interviewed by Philip Ingram on Times Radio’s Front Line at day 1,400 of Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, argued that U.S.-led diplomacy has cycled repeatedly: Washington pressures Kyiv, crafts a more reasonable settlement, presents it to Moscow, and Russia rejects it while claiming progress. He described proximity talks in Florida involving Russians, Americans, Europeans and Ukrainians, said Putin still demands full capitulation, and claimed Trump is being played by Putin through business-deal promises involving special envoy Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, Marco Rubio, and Kremlin officials such as Sergei Lavrov and Kirill Dmitriev. Volker cited sanctions on Russian oil and gas, lack of secondary sanctions, weak action against the shadow fleet, insufficient pressure on oil prices, and the need for uninterrupted paid-for Western arms, lifted weapons-use restrictions, and long-range strikes on Russian military targets. He also pointed to Ukraine’s attacks on shadow-fleet tankers, a Black Sea Kilo-class submarine, oil refineries and storage, an alleged GRU officer death, drone innovation, Prigozhin’s Wagner march as evidence of Russian apathy, and Russia’s economic strain—budget, inflation, interest rates, labor shortages, discounted oil—supporting his prediction of a possible 2026 ceasefire but not a durable peace, with deterrence and EU/NATO 5% GDP defense spending central.
