## Summary

Mark Galeotti argues that Russia’s accelerating internet repression—mobile-data throttling, site blocks, VPN targeting, and app-payment restrictions—is being driven less by Putin personally than by security agencies trying to blunt Ukrainian drone signals, opposition mobilization, and VPN access to foreign news. He frames Putin as a 19th-century geopolitician obsessed with great-power status and coercive power, compares his trajectory to Ivan the Terrible’s self-destructive late reign, and says the war is burning Russia’s future: oil/gas-funded legitimacy, sovereign wealth, economic dynamism, technological talent, and the everyday digital convenience that Moscow and provincial users depend on through WhatsApp, Yandex, Telegram, and mobile payments. The discussion also links Russia’s drone and electronic-warfare adaptation to elite conflict between Rostec/Sergey Chemezov’s top-down, contract-driven gigantism and bottom-up innovation around the Rubicon drone unit and Defense Minister Andrey Belousov, while contrasting Ukraine’s grassroots, data-driven defense-tech ecosystem with Rheinmetall CEO Armin Papperger’s dismissive comments and the challenge of scaling innovation without letting incumbents squeeze competition.
