## Summary

Robert Siegel, David Brooks, and E.J. Dionne assessed Donald Trump’s standing nearly a year into his second term, citing down polls, Republican losses in major 2025 elections, the longest government shutdown ever, the East Wing demolition, and Indiana Republicans’ redistricting defiance as signs of “decay but not fracture.” Brooks said Trump still had a majority advantage over Joe Biden in some earlier polling and was exercising power with fewer restraints than any modern president, while Dionne argued Trump had lost a “reasonable majority,” citing Gallup/AP-NORC approval around 36% and claims that up to a quarter of Trump voters had moved away because of cost-of-living anger, billionaire imagery, immigration overreach, and perceived indifference to economic pain. They linked the political mood to affordability, anxiety, loneliness, and pessimism, noting Democrats’ focus on tariffs, health-insurance subsidies, and price pressures, while Republicans largely denied a crisis; they cited data points including inflation around 3%, record median wages, median income around $88,000, and persistent pain in housing, health care, child care, transportation, cars, higher education, and electricity costs tied partly to AI data centers. They also discussed AI as a potentially transformative but uncertain technology producing bubbles, expert optimism/pessimism, stock-market support, and public fear; the Trump administration’s national security strategy’s harsh language about Europe, immigration, and the far right; the World Values Survey’s individualism/traditionalism framework; rising anti-Semitism after the Brown University and Bondi Beach shootings; and the distinction between criticism of Israeli policy and anti-Semitism, while noting mass shootings were at a 20-year low.
