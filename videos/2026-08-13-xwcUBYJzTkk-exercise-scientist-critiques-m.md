## Summary

Dr. Mike Israetel of RP Strength reviews footage of Magnus Midtbø, a former pro rock climber turned YouTuber, doing feats of strength and explains why they're possible. Midtbø, at about 165 lbs, reps the full stack on a lat pulldown or row machine next to Larry Wheels with strict full-range form. He also does a one-arm muscle-up, clap pull-ups, crushing and pinch grip challenges, and a record 75.5 lb grip lift that beat the previous 73 lb record. In the Norwegian military pull-up test (11.25 kg added), he did well past the long-standing 23-rep record. Mike gives several reasons for this strength. First, strength is partly a skill, so a nervous system trained for vertical pulling matters a lot. Second, tendon insertion points farther from the joint may give more leverage. Third, Midtbø has a brachial artery of 4.4 mm, roughly femoral-artery size, so he likely clears metabolites and delivers oxygen faster, which would explain his endurance at high force without bulky muscle. The video also covers a tight-squeeze cave crawl, urban bouldering, and sponsor reads for the RP Hypertrophy app and Element electrolytes.

For your own training, the main point is specificity. To get good at a skill like muscle-ups, train the skill itself several times a week, and also train its components. For muscle-ups that means heavy weighted pull-ups done explosively, heavy weighted dips with a powerful push out of the bottom, and the muscle-up itself. Mike says explosive clap pull-ups give no special edge for size or strength over simply adding weight to a belt, so do them only if you want that skill. He also notes that standardized, strict-form testing, such as weighted pull-ups at a fixed standard, shows what people can really do, since loose-form rep claims often don't hold up. Don't expect Midtbø-level results from copying his lifts, because much of his ability comes from genetics (artery size, tendon leverage) and years of climbing. Treat him as inspiration, and build strength through progressive weighted pulling and pushing plus practice of your target skill.
