## Summary

An emergency medicine doctor presents five factors that determine healthy aging: metabolic health (insulin resistance driven by visceral fat, poor sleep, and processed foods), muscle mass (loss causes falls, disability, and metabolic dysfunction), daily movement (especially walking after meals to control blood sugar), brain health (protected by metabolic control, sleep, exercise, and social connection—40% of dementia is preventable), and nervous system regulation (chronic stress accelerates aging through elevated cortisol disrupting sleep, metabolism, and decision-making). The central argument is that the 92-year-old woman dancing at midnight thrives because she maintained these factors for decades, while people struggling at 50 neglected them; the difference emerges from small, consistent choices rather than extreme protocols.

To shift your trajectory: build muscle through resistance training two to three times weekly (bodyweight exercises, bands, or weights work equally); eat 20-30g protein per meal, totaling 1.2-1.6g per kilogram of body weight daily; walk 15 minutes after meals to blunt blood sugar spikes; prioritize seven to nine hours of quality sleep; maintain meaningful social bonds; treat hearing loss with hearing aids; do both aerobic and strength work; manage stress through extended-exhale breathing (exhale longer than inhale); limit alcohol; and weave movement into daily life rather than reserving exercise for structured sessions. Start with what you can do now and progress gradually—the second-best time to begin is today.
