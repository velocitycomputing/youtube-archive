## Summary

Russia faces a critical manpower shortage, losing over 30,000 soldiers monthly while recruiting only ~27,000, creating an unsustainable deficit that forces mobilization. The landscape has shifted dramatically toward drone warfare: Ukraine achieves an 8:1 casualty ratio by deploying cheap FPV drones ($300 each) against Russian infantry, while Russia attempts to replace soldiers at $100,000 per year—a cost differential Ukrainians have weaponized deliberately. Russian recruitment is deteriorating due to corruption, minimal training, and demoralization; military bloggers describe it as a "conveyor belt of losses," with older, unfit recruits and conscripted prisoners replacing motivated volunteers. Mobilization appears imminent within months, despite Putin's previous agreement with citizens to shield them from war's impact.

For observers: mobilization signals Russia will shift to universal conscription and a wartime economy, pulling hundreds of thousands from the workforce—a serious economic gamble with uncertain payoff. The underlying problem is structural: Russia can generate soldiers through conscription, but quality training requires resources and time that aren't being invested, meaning larger numbers won't solve the casualty rate without economic disruption. Ukraine's manpower challenge operates differently due to clear defensive purpose and drone-force multiplication, making it less dependent on raw troop quality.
