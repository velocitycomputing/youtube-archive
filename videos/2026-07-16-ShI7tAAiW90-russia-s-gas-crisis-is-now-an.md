## Summary

**What was discussed:**
Russia faces a cascading economic crisis extending far beyond fuel, triggered by Ukrainian drone strikes that destroyed roughly 50% of refining capacity. Despite producing massive quantities of oil and gas, the Russian government artificially keeps fuel prices cheap through subsidies, creating rationing (30 liters per customer in some regions). This shortage has triggered a transportation crisis: freight costs for grain rose 20–30% in days, food prices in stores spiked, and small farmers cannot afford diesel. The core problem is Russia's geography—with one-fifth of the world's land area, shipping costs dominate food affordability across ultra-remote regions. The crisis has cascaded into household collapse: 81% of Russians are cutting food consumption, half a million filed for bankruptcy, wages are falling month-to-month despite declining inflation, and a banking crisis is emerging as loans default. Official GDP forecasts show just 0.4% growth (likely recession).

**What's actionable for the user:**
If you follow geopolitics or Russian economics, recognize that Ukraine's infrastructure targeting is more economically effective than direct conflict, and that Russia's official statistics mask a faster deterioration (deflating food inflation masks collapsing household purchasing power). If you hold investments or assess risk in Russia or connected economies, the banking crisis is the next domino—household bankruptcy cascades to bank solvency. For policy observers: Russia's size, which has historically been a strategic asset, has become a liability under economic stress, limiting Moscow's ability to maintain affordability across its territory without unsustainable subsidies.
