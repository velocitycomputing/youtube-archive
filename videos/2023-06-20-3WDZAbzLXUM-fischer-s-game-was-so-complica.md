## Summary

This video covers Bobby Fischer's win over Robert Byrne (not his brother Donald, whom Fischer beat in the "Game of the Century") from the 1963–64 US Championship, which Fischer won with a perfect 11/11. Fischer played a King's Indian setup as Black. After Byrne's Queen to D2 and the exchanges on e5, Byrne played the "wrong rook" to d1 (Rook f1–d1 rather than a1–d1). That let Fischer sacrifice a knight on f2, then play Knight to g4 and Knight takes e3, followed by the "star move" Knight takes g2. Byrne resigned on move 21, and two grandmasters commenting on the game in another room thought Byrne was winning even then. The presenter also shows that the correct Rook a1–d1 would have left Black with a good but not clearly winning game. In that line Fischer had prepared Queen to d7, ideas with Knight to f3 check, and Queen to b5. The video ends with the tournament standings: Larry Evans second with 7.5, then Benko with 7, and Donald Byrne last with 2.5. It also includes Hans Kmoch's joke that Evans won the tournament and Fischer only won the exhibition.

The video says that choice decided this game and that it is hard to judge even with the principles in mind.
