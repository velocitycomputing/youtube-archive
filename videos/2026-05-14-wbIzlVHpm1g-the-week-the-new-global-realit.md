## Summary

George Friedman and Christian Smith argued that a new global order was becoming visible through three linked shifts: Vladimir Putin’s reported call with Donald Trump, in which Putin allegedly offered a U.S.-Russia financial arrangement and said the Ukraine war would end soon; Putin’s turn toward Europe for investment and mediation, including naming Gerhard Schröder, which Kaja Kallas dismissed; and Trump’s upcoming Beijing summit with Xi Jinping, described as a blessing of a deal already being negotiated in South Korea. Friedman claimed Russia is economically exhausted, cannot print money without worsening inflation, and is admitting defeat in Ukraine; that Europe is not a unified political actor and must decide whether it can act as a power or be divided by Russia; and that China needs U.S. trade because it is the world’s second-largest economy but ranks only 71st in per-capita GDP, while the U.S. wants cheaper goods and a non-offensive Chinese military. He also claimed Xi had purged army and navy leadership after a possible attempted coup, that a Taiwan settlement may involve guaranteed autonomy, and that Iran’s Hormuz Strait pressure is aimed at disrupting the U.S.-China reset despite China not materially backing Iran.

For follow-up, the concrete leads are: monitor whether the Trump-Xi summit is held and whether a pre-summit agreement is disclosed; track Russia-Europe signals around Schröder, Nord Stream, Rosneft, German energy policy, and whether Western Europe splits from Poland, the Baltics, Finland, and Romania; watch Chinese military purges, Taiwan-related KMT messaging, and U.S. guarantees of autonomy; and assess Iran/Hormuz shipping risk, insurance costs, and whether China’s stance weakens Tehran. If the user is making investment, supply-chain, energy, or policy decisions, the key questions are whether tariffs ease and prices fall, whether Europe increases defense spending and energy independence, whether Poland and the Baltics remain U.S.-aligned buffers, and whether U.S.-China normalization reduces conflict risk while marginalizing Iran. Research leads include Friedman’s “Storm Before the Calm,” Geopolitical Futures’ prior Ukraine and Taiwan episodes, and primary reporting on the South Korea U.S.-China talks, China’s military command changes, and any formal Russia-Ukraine ceasefire terms.
