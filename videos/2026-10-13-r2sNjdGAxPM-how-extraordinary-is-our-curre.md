## Summary

Geopolitical Futures chairman George Friedman, interviewed by Christian Smith, framed the current moment as a normal geopolitical transition rather than an unprecedented crisis, comparing it to World War I, World War II, the Cold War, and earlier Iran hostage-era tensions. The discussion centered on whether the United States can prevent Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons, whether the “Mecca Accords” among Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan will become a real regional force, how much influence Iran has lost, and whether the Houthis in Yemen and attacks in the Strait of Hormuz/Red Sea can be neutralized without U.S. ground troops. Friedman claimed air power alone cannot destroy Iran’s IRGC, that Iran may still obtain a nuclear weapon but deterrence and regional pressure matter, and that the most motivated actors against Iranian nuclear capability are Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan rather than the United States. He also addressed U.S. relations with Russia, China, and Europe: arguing the U.S. is shifting burden to Europe, that the EU is the second-largest economy and capable of confronting Russia if it chooses, that China is primarily an economic rather than military threat, that U.S.-China war is unlikely, and that Europe’s rearmament and U.S. troop moves from Germany to Poland/Baltics are rational. Key data included gasoline over $5 a gallon, Turkey as NATO’s second-largest military, Pakistan as a nuclear power, China as second-largest economy but 71st in per capita income, and roughly 10,000 U.S. soldiers in Poland and the Baltics.
