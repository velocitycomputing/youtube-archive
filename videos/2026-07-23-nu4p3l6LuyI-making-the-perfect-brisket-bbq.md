## Summary

Franklin conducted a controlled brisket experiment cooking three identical Prime briskets using different wrapping methods—foil (Texas Crutch), butcher paper, and unwrapped—to isolate the effect of each on moisture, bark formation, and texture. The episode covered fundamentals including trimming for fat balance, applying a simple salt-and-pepper rub with coarse pepper to attract smoke, cooking at 275–285°F in an offset smoker, and the "split cook" method (cook to 165°F internal, rest overnight, finish next morning) to avoid overnight vigils. Discussion included keeping a log to track variables and emphasized that changing only one variable at a time prevents confusion. The three methods yielded distinct results: foil-wrapped stayed moist but tasted pot-roasty; butcher paper balanced moisture with pronounced bark and beef flavor; unwrapped developed maximum crust with stronger smoke character but less beef taste.

To replicate these results, cook your brisket at 275–285°F until it feels soft and tender, breaking under its own weight with minimal force to slice. If you need sleep, use the split-cook method—cook to 165°F internal temperature, add a large log, rest overnight, then finish cooking. Keep a detailed log of each cook (wood type, temperature swings, rub, placement) so you can identify what worked when changing variables. Pick one wrapping style and master it before experimenting; paper wrapping appears optimal for balancing moisture and bark. Use coarse pepper in your rub, trim conservatively to retain cooking fat, and accept that brisket requires 12+ hours of low-and-slow cooking—there is no shortcut to the bark and tenderness that define quality brisket.
