## Summary

Pierre Poilievre, Canada’s Conservative leader, argued that the United States is making a strategic mistake by moving away from allies such as Canada, the UK, and NATO, citing Trump’s Davos-era remarks about Greenland and Canada becoming the 51st state as evidence of an adversarial posture. He claimed Canada is a resource superpower with the world’s fourth-largest oil reserves—behind Venezuela, Saudi Arabia, and Iran, ahead of Iraq and the U.S.—and that Canada is the most reliable supplier because it works with U.S. refineries, sells at a discount, and could build a strategic reserve if the Strait of Hormuz were disrupted. He tied this to demands for tariff-free Canadian access to U.S. markets for steel, aluminum, lumber, and automobiles, plus cooperation on strategic minerals. On Iran, he said the regime is hostile, sponsored terrorism, shot down PS752 killing 55 Canadian citizens and 30 permanent residents, and is enriching uranium for weapons; he judged the initial U.S. strikes necessary to prevent nuclear capability, opposed Canadian ground troops, and compared the ideological risk to North Korea. He said he had not met Trump, supported Prime Minister Carney’s backing of the Iran strikes but not Canadian troops, and avoided public criticism of Carney during trade negotiations. He also linked U.S.-Canada friction to domestic Canadian anger over taxes, regulation, housing, wages, and 20,000 immigrant doctors unable to practice, and shared a personal adoption/working-class background involving Marlene and Donald, a half-brother Patrick, a biological mother who placed him for adoption at 16, and a biological father in British Columbia.
