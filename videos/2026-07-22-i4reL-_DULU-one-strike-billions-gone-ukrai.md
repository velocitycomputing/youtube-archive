## Summary

Ukraine conducted its second major strike on Russian Wildberries logistics facilities in four days, destroying goods worth $1.9-3 billion and crippling a company that controls 45% of Russia's online shopping market. President Zelenskyy framed the strikes as strategically "returning the war to Russia" to force the Kremlin toward diplomacy, targeting both military supply chains and civilian economic infrastructure. Simultaneously, Ukraine is steadily degrading Russian air defense systems—destroying SAM systems and radars daily—forcing Russia to spread its limited defenses across too many priorities: military installations, the Kremlin, oil facilities, the Kerch Bridge, and now logistics hubs. The analysis notes that Ukraine deliberately avoids targeting Russian civilians despite having the capability, instead using economic pressure (fuel shortages, business closures, rising prices) to potentially turn Russian public opinion against Putin. Additionally, Ukraine announced a major military leadership change, replacing Commander-in-Chief General Surskyy with General Mykhailo Drapatyi; Russian military analysts view this as bad news for Moscow, though the immediate tactical impact on the front lines is likely to be minimal.

**For the user:** This strategy demonstrates how Ukraine is using drone technology and economic targeting to sustain pressure on Russia without resorting to civilian casualties—a calculated approach designed to erode both Russia's military capacity and domestic support for the war. If you're tracking this conflict or involved in policy discussions, note that Russia's critical vulnerability is not primarily military at this point but economic and political: the Russian public's response to shortages and price increases may prove more destabilizing than any single military defeat. If interested in supporting Ukrainian efforts, the video mentions Car for Ukraine as an active organization delivering vehicles and supporting wounded warriors.
