## Summary

Justin Clunk documents his international guitar tour across Asia and Australia, performing for large audiences in cities including Beijing, Shanghai, Mumbai, Singapore, Brisbane, Melbourne, and Sydney. He discusses the challenge of shows spaced far apart—eleven flights and significant gaps between performances make it difficult for the band to develop rhythm and confidence in their material. He notes stark cultural differences in audience behavior: Chinese audiences are quiet and reserved (which he learns signals respect and engagement), while Indian audiences are expected to be energetic and enthusiastic. The vlog captures venue setups, band dynamics during downtime, and reflections on how travel exhaustion and irregular performance schedules affect the touring experience.

If you're involved in live performance, touring, or working with international audiences, the key insight is that show spacing and cultural audience expectations require different performance preparation strategies. Clunk's observation that back-to-back shows in Australia will help the band "lock in" suggests clustering performances to build momentum, while recognizing that audience behavior varies by region—silence doesn't mean disengagement, and what works in one market won't work in another.
