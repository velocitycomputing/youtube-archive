## Summary

Brandon Dion, founder of the 52 Week Guitar Player program, lists eight problems intermediate guitarists face and gives a fix for each. (1) They don't know what to practice because online content overwhelms them, so he says to go deep on one video's material and set clear goals. (2) They ask "should I learn CAGE, triads or modes?" when learning the thing would answer the question, and the only real cost is time. (3) Their solos sound dull. He says to play with emotion and add one new element, such as a neighboring pentatonic shape, to the box shape they already know. (4) They keep playing the same licks because they skim many topics instead of going deep. Pros spend months on one thing, and knowing the fretboard well enough to find any note in a second or less lets you target chord tones. (5) They struggle to memorize scales. The fix is many more reps (he suggests 1,000) and varied practice: starting on different strings or scale degrees, string skipping, and playing intervals. (6) They fear theory, which he says you only have to learn once and which is easier to relearn than lost speed. (7) They practice inconsistently. (8) They get lost when improvising because they don't know the chord progression well enough. He says to learn it as well as a favorite song. The video ends with a pitch for his paid program (enrollment through June 30 and a waiting list after).

For you, the most useful steps are these:
- Pick one lesson or topic and practice only that for weeks.
- Instead of asking whether to learn something like CAGE, triads or modes, just learn it and judge afterward.
- Add one new element, such as a neighboring pentatonic shape, to your solos and mix it with shapes you already know.
- Drill the fretboard notes until you can find any note in a second or less.
- Work on one scale at a time, aiming for about 1,000 reps. Vary the starting string and degree, and try string skipping and intervals, until you can visualize the scale before you play it.
- Practice every day.
- Before soloing over a backing track, learn its chord progression (usually about a minute long). Test yourself by starting the track, leaving the room, and finding your place when you return.

Treat the course pitch as marketing. None of the advice requires it.
