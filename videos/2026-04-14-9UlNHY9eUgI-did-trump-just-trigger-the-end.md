## Summary

Yanis Varoufakis and Wolfgang Münchau debated whether Trump’s “blockade of the blockade” of the Strait of Hormuz marks a Suez-like end of dollar hegemony, rejecting the analogy because Britain in 1956 was bankrupt and oil-dependent while the U.S. is solvent, less Gulf-oil dependent, and Wall Street banks are reporting more than $40 billion in first-quarter trading revenues; they noted Iran’s blockade affects about a quarter of global oil, non-Americans need roughly $1 trillion in dollars annually, about 20% more than U.S. defense spending, and $100 trillion of global debt is dollar-denominated, but they argued Hormuz accelerates an alternative architecture via Pakistan’s shift toward an electric economy, Iranian and Russian oil sold in yuan, China’s expanded CIPS system, BRICS pay, local-currency trade, BRICS enlargement, and the Ukraine-sanctions lesson that dollar exclusion can happen to others. They also discussed Hungary’s election: Péter Magyar’s landslide and two-thirds majority over Viktor Orbán, with vote share around 53% versus 39%, arguing Magyar is another right-wing, Eurosceptic “Brexiters without the exit” figure who will likely continue rent-extraction rather than mark a liberal-democratic turning point; they mentioned EU issues including the €90 billion Ukraine loan, Belgian PM Bart De Wever’s veto over Russian assets, John Bolton’s influence on blockade strategy, Freddie Sayers’ interview, and Ursula von der Leyen’s Brussels relief, while a mid-roll ad promoted Augusta Precious Metals and gold IRAs as retirement diversification.
