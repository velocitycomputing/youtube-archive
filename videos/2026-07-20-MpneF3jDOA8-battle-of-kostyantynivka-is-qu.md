## Summary

The battle for Kostyantynivka showcases how Ukraine is revolutionizing ground warfare through unmanned systems integration. Russia is executing a multi-week pincer movement through surrounding terrain, advancing 100 meters per day in a drone-heavy environment where troop movement is severely constrained by weather and aerial surveillance. Ukraine counters with specialized assault units like the 425th Regiment clearing Russian positions, while deploying Droid TW127EUGV ground robots that fix enemy troops in place using the "find, fix, finish" tactic: reconnaissance drones locate targets, ground robots suppress and pin them, then FPV strike drones eliminate them. Despite Russia's concentrated effort, Kostyantynivka remains extremely difficult to capture due to these layered defenses, and Ukraine is simultaneously degrading Russian supply lines through strikes on bridges and logistics routes.

For observers tracking the conflict, this battle signals that Russia's primary offensive has been accelerated—likely exploiting internal Ukrainian military friction between commander-in-chief Syrsky and Defence Minister Fedorov—but this political window may prove militarily ineffectual given the slow-paced nature of modern drone-constrained warfare. Monitor Kostyantynivka's status as a bellwether for whether Russia can sustain momentum against integrated Ukrainian drone-and-infantry defenses, and watch for expansion of ground robotics in assault roles beyond the current 5% of unmanned missions, as this represents a fundamental shift in how land warfare operates.
