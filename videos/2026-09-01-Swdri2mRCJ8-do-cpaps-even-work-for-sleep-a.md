## Summary

**What was discussed:** Obstructive sleep apnea affects roughly 1 billion people worldwide and increases risk of cardiovascular disease, hypertension, and poor sleep quality. CPAP machines—the current standard treatment costing ~$800+—work by using pressurized air to keep airways open. However, a 2022 HHS review of 52 studies found weak evidence that CPAP actually improves long-term health outcomes. The core problem: studies use inconsistent definitions and measurement methods, making comparison impossible. More critically, CPAP is highly *efficacious* (mechanically effective at preventing airway collapse) but has poor *effectiveness* in real-world use because only ~50% of people stick with it consistently. When researchers isolate patients who actually use CPAP as prescribed (4+ hours nightly), benefits like reduced cardiovascular events do emerge. Oral appliance therapy (OAT)—a mouth guard-like device—is less efficacious but achieves 95% adherence rates and comparable health outcomes for mild-to-moderate OSA, breaking even in cost within four months.

**What's actionable:** If diagnosed with sleep apnea, CPAP remains the gold-standard treatment, but if you struggle with the mask and discomfort, ask your doctor about oral appliance therapy—especially if you have mild-to-moderate OSA. The treatment that works best is whichever one you'll actually use consistently every night. Your doctor can assess your specific OSA severity and help determine whether OAT's lower efficacy is offset by its higher real-world adherence for your situation.
