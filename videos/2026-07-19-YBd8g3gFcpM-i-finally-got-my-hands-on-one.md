## Summary

The host acquired a 1970s Epiphone Scroll SC-550 guitar from Guitar Center and detailed its construction and specs. Produced 1976–1979 at Japan's Matsumokco factory, the Scroll came in three models: the SC-350 (mahogany/bolt-on, entry-level), SC-450 (maple/set neck), and SC-550 (deluxe with ebony fretboard, binding, mother-of-pearl block inlays, and gold hardware). This particular SC-550 features a distinctive German-carved scroll cutaway, a 5-piece maple back with 3-piece maple top and neck, 25.5" scale length, slim C-shaped neck profile, and Leo Quan-style wraparound bridge. The stock Epiphone humbuckers include a coil-split toggle and deliver bright, country-like tones that surprised the host positively. The guitar weighs 9 lbs 3 oz and feels neck-heavy due to the oversized scroll headstock.

If hunting vintage gear, look for these Matsumokco-made Scrolls as a unique alternative to modern imports—they're expensive but sonically solid and excellent if you appreciate Gibson RDs. Distinguish the SC-550 from often-mislabeled SC-450s by checking for ebony fretboard, mother-of-pearl block inlays, and binding. The entry-level SC-350 offers the scroll aesthetics at lower cost. Avoid these if you have back issues (neck-heavy), and be aware the original Japanese tuners lose rigidity when detuned, though they hold pitch adequately when tuning up.
