## Summary

China's recent AI model releases (Zhipu and Meituan) have shattered the American proprietary AI business model by demonstrating that open-source Chinese alternatives deliver comparable performance at roughly 10x lower cost. Eric Schmidt, architect of US chip sanctions, admitted the technology gap has narrowed from 1-2 years to 4-6 months, and 80% of American startups have already switched to Chinese open-source models for economic reasons. The US faces compounding vulnerabilities: dependence on Chinese supply chains for power infrastructure, domestic electricity shortages limiting data-center expansion, embedded spyware in US models (like Claude) that backfires by accelerating Chinese adoption, and a political establishment pursuing zero-sum strategies that ignore economic interdependence.

For practical decisions: if you're selecting AI systems, the cost differential makes Chinese open-source models economically rational for most non-cutting-edge applications—betting on proprietary US offerings at 10x the price is financially indefensible unless you need frontier capabilities. Geopolitical trade wars and export controls are failing to constrain China's progress and instead incentivizing local deployment of open-source alternatives. The structural fix requires acknowledging that US-China economic success is coupled, not competitive, and that affordability and supply-chain stability matter more than regulatory protectionism to ordinary businesses and consumers.
