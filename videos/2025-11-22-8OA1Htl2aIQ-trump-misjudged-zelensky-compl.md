## Summary

Retired Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling, interviewed by Bill Crystal, argued that the Trump administration’s reported 28-point Ukraine peace plan, presented by Secretary Rubio and Envoy Witkoff, misjudges Ukraine and Zelensky by treating the proposal as a U.S.-Russia arrangement that pressures Kyiv to cede territory, reduce its army, forgo NATO membership, recognize Russian language, and grant special status to the Russian Orthodox Church. Hertling said the plan reflects Putin’s talking points, rewards aggression, and ignores Ukrainian sovereignty, national identity, and historical trauma, citing his work with Ukrainian Col. Gen. Hennadiy Vorobyov, the Soviet-era corruption he saw in the Ukrainian military, and Anne Applebaum’s account of Stalin’s Holodomor. He also warned that frozen conflicts in Moldova/Transnistria, Armenia/Azerbaijan, Crimea/Donbas, and Georgia show Russia’s pattern of taking territory, pausing, and returning, and he linked the plan to broader U.S. signals—withdrawal of forces from Romania, a carrier strike group leaving the Mediterranean/Red Sea, and the NATO ambassador’s suggestion to give a senior post to Germany—that he said undermine a 70-year alliance and alarm 49 non-Belarusian European countries.

Actionable takeaways: treat the 28-point plan as a policy claim requiring verification—obtain the actual text, compare it with Ukrainian and European responses, and track whether it is merely a negotiating position or a U.S. ultimatum. Research leads include Hertling’s account of Ukrainian military reform, Applebaum’s Holodomor history, and the frozen-conflict cases he named; follow-up questions should ask whether Ukraine would survive security guarantees without NATO, whether territorial concessions create a repeatable Russian template, how Europe would respond if the U.S. pressures Kyiv, and whether Congress or European capitals can alter the plan. For decisions, the user should decide whether to support continued aid, demand transparency on the plan, or prioritize alliance cohesion; no direct personal health implications are presented, but the geopolitical risk to European security and U.S. credibility is the main consequence.
