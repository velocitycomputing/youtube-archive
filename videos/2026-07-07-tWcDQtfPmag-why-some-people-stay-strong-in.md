## Summary

Dr. Alex discusses how the real fear isn't dying young but losing independence in later years—a problem affecting many people in their 50s-60s whose bodies have deteriorated from accumulated "lifestyle debt." He explains that common conditions like high blood pressure and low muscle mass are wrongly accepted as "normal," and that our modern environment of abundant food and sedentary living doesn't match our evolved biology. The healthcare system treats disease symptoms with medications but cannot restore lost resilience; meanwhile, biological aging is determined not by years but by accumulated choices, with muscle mass being critical for blood sugar regulation, immune function, and fall prevention. Without deliberate resistance training after age 30, people lose 3-8% of muscle per decade, and by the time symptoms appear, the damage is often irreversible.

To preserve capability into old age, prioritize five fundamentals consistently: eat real food high in protein and fiber, perform 2-3 resistance training sessions weekly, do zone 2 cardiovascular training 1-2 times weekly (sustainable pace where you can still talk), incorporate daily movement beyond the gym, and protect sleep and stress management. Start building this "biological capital" now in your 30s-50s—waiting until 60 or 70 means the trajectory is already set and leverage is lost. Ignore expensive biohacks and supplements; weakness and dependence in later life are largely preventable through years of ordinary, consistent choices, not dramatic interventions. Your responsibility for long-term health begins decades earlier than most people realize.
