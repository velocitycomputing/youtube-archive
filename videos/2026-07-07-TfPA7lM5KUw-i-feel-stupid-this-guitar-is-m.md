## Summary

The guitarist explored an innovative 1989 Ibanez S72 belonging to Neural DSP CEO Doug Castro, which featured several unconventional modifications: lighter 9-gauge strings (versus his usual 10-gauge), fully scalloped frets throughout the neck, tuning down to E-flat, a 7.5-inch radius fretboard, and noiseless DiMarzio pickup pickups. Each innovation fundamentally changed his playing experience—lighter strings required less pressure, scalloped frets made bends effortless and revealed he was using excessive hand pressure, the flatter radius allowed easier access under strings, and the noiseless pickups eliminated the hum of traditional single-coils without sacrificing tone. He also highlighted Tyrese Brennan, a working guitarist with cubital nerve syndrome affecting his left hand, who needs costly physiotherapy and crowdfunding support.

If you experience hand fatigue or want to improve your playing, drop your string gauge to 9 or 9.5 (D'Addario makes intermediate sizes), experiment with E-flat tuning for a warmer Strat tone, and consider installing noiseless pickups like the DiMarzio Area 58 (~$100 USD) to eliminate hum at gigs without tone loss. More fundamentally, reassess how much pressure you're actually using—you likely don't need as much as you think, which matters for long-term hand health before issues like nerve compression become serious.
