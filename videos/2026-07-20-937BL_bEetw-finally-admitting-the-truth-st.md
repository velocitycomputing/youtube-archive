## Summary

Jordan and Kaylee shared their seven-year journey moving to Panama after their bus broke down in 2019. What began as a temporary stop in Boquete became a real estate purchase and dream home project, but they faced cascading crises: a $1,000 passport deportation fine, vehicle impoundment, extreme COVID-19 lockdowns (martial law-style restrictions limiting movement to specific hours), Hurricane Eta's catastrophic flooding, and multiple protest-driven shortages of gas, propane, and food lasting months at a time. These hardships, especially with a child, made them seriously consider leaving—they bought escape property in Vermont and contemplated return to the US repeatedly between 2021-2023.

Everything shifted around 2024 when Panama's political and social stability returned; gas, food, and services became reliably available, the town came alive with festivals and tourism, and they finally felt genuinely at home. If you're considering moving abroad or facing major life disruption, maintain a financial escape plan but couple it with gut-level intuition about whether to stay or go—don't make the decision on logic alone. Their actionable lesson: navigate valleys without abandoning your goal prematurely, but have a real exit if conditions truly warrant it; patience plus authentic knowing of what's right for your family matters more than external circumstances.
