## Summary

The video outlines 12 warning signs of pancreatic cancer, categorized as early whispers, intermediate signals, and late-stage alarms. Early subtle signs include early satiety (feeling full after a few bites), indigestion unresponsive to antacids, crushing fatigue despite adequate sleep, and appetite loss. More serious warnings are unexplained weight loss exceeding 5% in six months, new-onset diabetes after age 50 in otherwise healthy individuals, deep boring back pain unresponsive to stretching or medication, pale or greasy floating stools indicating malabsorption, and persistent nausea one hour after eating. The alarming triad—skin and eye yellowing (jaundice), spontaneous blood clots, and severe unexplained full-body itching—signal late-stage disease. The core message: pancreatic cancer is deadly because the organ sits deep with no early screening test, so symptoms appear elsewhere (stomach, back, skin, energy) and are easily dismissed as aging, stress, or minor conditions for months or years.

Contact your doctor urgently if you have: consistent early fullness with unintentional weight loss; monthly indigestion no longer controlled by antacids; persistent fatigue unrelieved by sleep; unexplained appetite loss; weight loss of 5%+ in six months; new diabetes diagnosis after 50 without typical risk factors (overweight, family history); deep back pain lasting weeks, worsening at night or after meals, unresponsive to usual remedies; stool changes (pale, clay-colored, floating, greasy, or distinctly foul) lasting over two weeks; nausea consistently appearing after meals; any yellowing of skin or whites of eyes (seek emergency care); unexplained clots; or severe itching lasting weeks. The action threshold: if you're over 50 with two or more symptoms together, or symptoms worsening, schedule an appointment now—early detection dramatically improves survival.
