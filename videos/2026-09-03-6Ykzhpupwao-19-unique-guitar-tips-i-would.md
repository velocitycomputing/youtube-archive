## Summary

Brandon D'Eon, a guitar instructor with about 15 years of playing and a music degree from St. Francis Xavier University, lists 19 tips he'd give his younger self (the title and intro say 18, but he counts to 19). The basics: stay physically relaxed while practicing (no tension in the forearm, shoulders, traps, neck or stomach), because you perform the way you practice; practice with a metronome; learn the notes of the fretboard so you don't freeze when improvising; and play with a legato (smooth, connected) tone rather than a choppy staccato one. On approach, he says to:
- pick a specific goal;
- practice consistently;
- copy and transcribe the players you admire, then add your own spin;
- put accuracy before speed;
- push the last 10% of a song to get it right;
- accept that there are no tricks, only practice;
- mess up in front of people to beat stage fright;
- learn whole songs rather than parts.

He also says that:
- playing songs makes you an entertainer, not a musician, so you need to know your instrument and how music works;
- noodling on what you already know isn't practice, which needs a clear objective;
- jamming with others exposes gaps in your playing;
- things take longer than you expect, though learning speeds up over time;
- theory is easier than its reputation;
- you should track progress (for example, the BPM you can reach on a given exercise);
- you should try to work out a problem yourself before asking for help or quitting.

The video also plugs his 52-week guitar program.

For you, the most useful steps are these:
- **Set a goal and a routine.** Write down one specific goal and practice a little every day.
- **Practice slowly with a metronome.** Run each exercise at a tempo where you can play it cleanly and relaxed. Check your shoulders, neck and forearm for tension as you go.
- **Keep a notebook.** Log each exercise and the BPM you reached. His example is major 7th arpeggios in every key, with a BPM for each.
- **Learn songs fully.** Pick one solo or song by a player you like, transcribe it, and learn all of it, including the last 10% that most people skip.
- **Fill the gaps.** Spend time on fretboard note names and basic theory.
- **Play with others.** Jam, or play for people, even if you make mistakes.
- **Stop noodling.** Treat any session without a stated objective as warm-up, not practice.
