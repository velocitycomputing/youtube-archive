## Summary

Anduril Industries announced the Steel Fence/Steelfin surface-to-air missile interceptor at AUSA, presenting it as a modular, cheaper alternative to legacy air-defense missiles: different first-stage boosters for different threats, a common second-stage airframe, and interchangeable seekers/warheads. The transcript names Anduril, founded in 2017 by Palmer Luckey, who previously sold Oculus VR to Facebook for about $2 billion in 2014, and references Anduril’s Lattice software, border surveillance towers, autonomous systems, and counter-drone work. It compares Steel Fence to Patriot PAC-1/PAC-2 track-via-missile guidance and PAC-3 active-radar guidance, and to Navy SM-2/SM-3/SM-6, noting proven Lockheed Martin and RTX track records versus Anduril’s faster software/manufacturing pitch. Key claims include compatibility with the Army’s M903A2 Patriot launcher, Navy Mark 41 VLS, and Typhon container system; open architecture; off-the-shelf components; and possible faster reconfiguration—new missile variants in about six weeks instead of months or years. The speaker cautions that missiles are sealed in containers, so field swapping of seekers/warheads is unlikely, and that unproven interceptors could become “dead weight” in VLS tubes if commanders prefer proven systems. The discussion extends to Typhon containers on littoral combat ships, a “Recap Ship” unmanned modular vessel idea with up to 18 containers and 74 missile cells, distributed air defense around Taiwan or Europe, and the Strait of Hormuz escort problem tied to the retirement of Oliver Hazard Perry frigates and LCS air-defense limitations.

Actionable takeaways are mostly defense/procurement research leads rather than health implications: watch for independent test data on Steel Fence’s intercept success against drones, cruise missiles, ballistic missiles, and maneuvering/hypersonic threats, and compare its kill reliability, unit cost, production rate, and VLS compatibility with Patriot and SM-series systems. If evaluating adoption, ask whether it can be fired from Mark 41/Typhon without launcher modifications, whether it retains secondary surface-attack capability like SM-2, how open architecture works in practice, whether new seekers/warheads can be fielded quickly, and how it integrates with Lattice, Aegis, Link-16, or other battle-management networks. For investment, policy, or wargaming analysis, follow up on Anduril’s actual test schedule, customer list, export interest from wealthy allies such as Saudi Arabia, and whether attritable unmanned ships or containerized Typhon barges could realistically provide escort air defense in contested straits. The key decision point is whether Steel Fence delivers enough capability at low enough cost and high enough reliability to justify displacing proven interceptors in limited launch-cell inventories.
