## Summary

Dr. Mike Israetel of RP Strength reviews the diets of several fitness YouTubers, with a lot of joking along the way. Sam Sulek's breakfast of a gallon of whole milk plus donuts has only 29g of protein, which Mike says is too little for his size and drug use. He'd want about 50g per meal, or even 100g when the meal is that large and high in fat. Mike's main argument is that total calories and adequate protein account for roughly 70–80% of diet outcomes. Carb/fat ratio, digestion speed and micronutrients matter progressively less for short-term fat loss or muscle gain, though they matter more for long-term health. He compares a bad day to a zero on one quiz in a semester: it drags the grade down, but staying within about 500 calories of your goal with enough protein is still a win. He mocks the Trenbalone Twins' "don't track anything" approach and their ghee and beef-heavy meals. He rates Lean Beef Patty's macro-friendly meals well, apart from her cottage cheese. Jeff Nippard's 5–10% surplus bulk (about 2,800 maintenance calories raised to roughly 3,000) gets a 10/10 as sound science. Chris Haria's 2,010-calorie day (176g protein) is judged too low for a 170 lb person trying to gain. Mike says Haria himself warned people not to copy it, and that the principles are what to take away.

For you, the actionable part is to set your calories first and hit your protein target every day, since that covers most of the result. Haria's day sits around 2,000 calories at 170 lb, and Mike says gaining at that weight usually takes about 2,500–3,000. For a lean bulk, raise your maintenance calories by only 5–10% (for example 2,800 to about 3,000), then adjust up or down based on how fast your weight changes. Don't let a chaotic or travel day make you abandon the plan. Eat enough protein and get within about 500 calories of target, using convenient options like protein shakes. On big, high-fat meals, consider more protein per meal, since digestion is slower. Fit in micronutrient-rich foods and some junk food when your calories and protein are already covered. Treat Haria's meal plan as a set of principles rather than a template, because it's too low in calories for most people trying to build muscle. Mike also promotes his RP Diet Coach app, which can track calories and protein only.
