## Summary

Mark Knopfler's "Sultans of Swing" solo achieves its genius through three-note triads—not flashy technique. A triad consists of a root note, a third interval above it, and a fifth interval. Each triad has three inversions (root form, first inversion, second inversion) that can be played across different string sets on the fretboard. The key insight is that Knopfler transforms plain triads into musical licks by adding one or two extra notes (like the fourth, sixth, or seventh) and using phrasing techniques like bends, slides, and rakes. He demonstrates this throughout "Sultans of Swing" over the chord progression D minor–C–B flat–A–F, using triads to lock to chord changes while making them sound melodic and emotional.

Learn all three inversions of triads on all six string set combinations (EAD, ADG, DGB, etc.), since the B string's different tuning creates unique shapes. Practice moving these inversions up and down the fretboard until you can access any triad in any position. Start applying the "cheat code" by taking one triad, adding a single extra note (bend a fourth up to the fifth, add a suspended fourth, etc.), and using phrasing to make it sound complete. Work through inversions of two chords (like A major and D minor) with string-skipping patterns. Transfer these tools to your own solos by using triads to track chord changes and weaving in scale-based ideas as you gain fluency.
