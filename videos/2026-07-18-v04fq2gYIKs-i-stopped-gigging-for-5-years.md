## Summary

The speaker discusses his five-year hiatus from live gigging after performing professionally since age six. He traces how live music evolved from enjoyable to obligatory work due to unchanged pay, growing personal responsibilities (relationships, potential family), and the increasing physical toll of touring. COVID became the inflection point that forced him to reconsider music as his sole income source. Contrary to expectations, he didn't actually miss the stage buzz, validation, or money—what he genuinely missed were the people, camaraderie, and especially playing outdoor festivals. He also challenges the concept of "professional musician" as an arbitrary, meaningless label, noting that many respected musicians throughout history earned their primary living elsewhere while creating their best work.

The practical value here is recognizing that stepping away from monetized performance isn't career suicide or failure—it's often necessary recovery. If you're burning out on music as an income source, consider reducing financial pressure temporarily or pivoting to playing purely for enjoyment (like the speaker did with casual gigs for his friend's band). The actionable insight: separate the art from the finances. Many successful musicians across genres (classical, rock, studio work) support themselves through multiple income streams without shame. If you're feeling trapped by the need to "stay professional," giving yourself permission to earn money elsewhere while playing for pure enjoyment can actually restore what made music valuable in the first place.
