## Summary

Dr. Leonid Kim discusses reversing brain aging through neuroplasticity and evidence-based habits, citing the COSMOS Web Trial of over 3,500 older adults randomized to Centrum Silver multivitamin or placebo for 3 years, with the multivitamin group showing better immediate recall and about 3 years of preserved memory function; the trial was supported by Centrum’s maker Halion, formerly Pfizer Consumer Healthcare, but conducted by independent researchers at Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Columbia University. He also covers choline’s role in acetylcholine production and neuronal membrane integrity, citing a Japanese randomized trial where 300 mg egg-yolk choline daily for 12 weeks improved verbal memory, and a Finnish observational study of about 2,500 men over 22 years where the highest phosphatidylcholine intake was associated with a 28% lower dementia risk. He discusses blueberries’ anthocyanins and nitric oxide effects, citing a double-blind trial of 26 g freeze-dried wild blueberry powder for 12 weeks improving immediate recall and task switching, plus a meta-analysis of 9 randomized trials with over 500 participants showing memory benefits in mild cognitive impairment. Finally, he cites a 2011 PNAS randomized trial where older adults doing 40–45 minutes of treadmill aerobic exercise three times weekly at 60–75% maximum heart rate for 1 year had a 2% increase in hippocampal volume, especially anterior hippocampus, and higher BDNF.

The user could consider starting a daily multivitamin, not necessarily Centrum Silver, while checking quality, nutrient gaps, medication interactions, and clinician guidance; add choline-rich foods such as 1–2 eggs daily, or salmon, tuna, cod, beef, chicken, or soybeans, with supplements only if needed; add about one cup of blueberries daily, fresh, frozen, or powder, especially if interested in prevention or mild cognitive concerns; and prioritize moderate aerobic exercise three times weekly for 40–45 minutes at 60–75% maximum heart rate using walking, cycling, elliptical, rowing, or jogging. Health implications include potential support for memory, focus, processing speed, hippocampal volume, BDNF, blood flow, and lower dementia risk, though observational findings are associative and individual results vary. Useful research leads include the COSMOS Web Trial, egg-yolk choline RCT, Finnish phosphatidylcholine cohort, blueberry powder RCT and meta-analysis, and the 2011 PNAS aerobic exercise hippocampus study; follow-up questions include whether the user has hypertension, diabetes, mobility limits, anticoagulant use, vitamin deficiencies, or early cognitive symptoms, and whether baseline cognitive testing or a supervised exercise plan is appropriate.
