## Summary

The instructor explained that scales are sequences of notes, with the major scale as the foundational "mother scale" from which all others derive. The seven modes of the major scale (Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, and Locrian) use identical notes but start and end on different root notes, each producing a distinct emotional quality—major/happy (Ionian), mysterious (Dorian), exotic (Phrygian), spacey/dreamy (Lydian), psychedelic (Mixolydian), sad/minor (Aeolian), and dissonant (Locrian). The instructor also covered pentatonic scales (five-note variants), particularly the major and minor pentatonic, which dominate blues and rock music.

Choose scales based on the emotional vibe you want, not theory alone. Start with major and minor pentatonic scales since they're most practical and cover most professional needs. Practice these over backing tracks or loops to train your ear, and trust what sounds right rather than forcing theoretical correctness—break the rules when it serves the music. You don't need to master all seven modes; focus on Ionian, Dorian, Mixolydian, and Aeolian as your core toolkit, then expand selectively based on your style.
