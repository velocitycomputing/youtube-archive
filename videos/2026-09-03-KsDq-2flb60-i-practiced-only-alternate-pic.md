## Summary

Guitarist "Lee" (host of the 52-Week Guitar Player program) spent 30 days practicing only alternate picking, aiming for clean 16th notes at 180 BPM. He ran harmonic minor modes with three notes per string in all 12 keys, warmed up from about 130 BPM upward, filmed C harmonic minor each day, and averaged 52 minutes 11 seconds per session. He drew ten lessons from it:

1. Pick slanting didn't help with odd note groupings (three or five per string), and thinking about it made picking harder.
2. Different muscles work at slow and fast tempos. He wasn't fully relaxed at his limit, got shoulder pain early on that faded, and was more relaxed at 160 by the end.
3. Practice faster than your goal tempo. He went past 180 on days 26–29, which made 170–180 feel easier.
4. Keep the picking window small (economy of motion).
5. Repetition without correction is pointless. Reps need refinement.
6. At high speed you must track the picking direction for each string. Turning around at the high E makes it five notes there, and the directions stay fixed.
7. Fifteen minutes a day is enough to maintain speed, but not to improve it.
8. Actually move your right hand. Sweat made it stick to the guitar, so he sometimes wore a wristband.
9. Use a light grip and a light touch. Hold the pick just tight enough that it doesn't fall out, and pick just hard enough to make sound.
10. Expect to sweat, and breathe deeply (in through the nose, out through the mouth).

He says he didn't really hit his goal. He played 185 cleanly on day 27, but only 1–2 of every 10 takes were good, he needs a ~20-minute warm-up, and the gains were specific to that one scale pattern and didn't carry over to other licks. His conclusion is that skill growth takes longer than people expect, and the only real shortcut is feedback from someone more advanced. He also promotes his 52-Week Guitar Player program, which reopens June 11 to the waiting list and June 15 to the public, with 100 spots.

If you want to work on alternate picking, here is a routine based on his. Play a three-notes-per-string scale or mode in all 12 keys. Warm up with 10–20 minutes of stepping the tempo up (130, 140, and so on), then push to your max tempo. Spend some time above your target speed, then bring it back down so the target feels easy. Record yourself and fix specific mistakes, such as wrong stroke direction, bumping adjacent strings, a wide picking window, or a tight grip. Keep your pick grip and picking force light, let your right hand move, and ease off if you feel pain. Do at least 15 minutes a day to maintain your speed, and about 50 minutes or more to improve it. Practice several scale and lick patterns, because gains from one pattern don't transfer automatically. Set realistic expectations, and get feedback from a stronger player or teacher. The free PDF and the program waiting list are both linked in the video description.
