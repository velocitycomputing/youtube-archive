## Summary

The video is a guided tour of the Forestiere Underground Gardens in Fresno, California, led by a docent. Baldassare Forestiere came from Sicily in the early 1900s to grow citrus. He bought land that had only a few inches of good topsoil over 3–5 feet of "hardpan", a rock-hard sedimentary layer that citrus roots can't penetrate. After a record 115°F summer in 1906, he began digging a cellar to sleep in. Over about 40 years he expanded it by hand, with pickaxes, shovels and wheelbarrows, to roughly 20 acres of rooms, passageways and courtyards. About four acres survive today. He broke up the hardpan and reused it as stone, and built Roman-style arches on forms he made himself. He had about a fourth-grade education. The complex includes kitchens, a dining room with a table that doubles as a planter, summer and winter bedrooms (the winter one has a fireplace with a shared flue), a fish pond, a bathtub, a chapel, and a bell that worked as a doorbell. Rooms sit 10–25 feet down, where it is 10–25°F cooler than the surface. Skylights, including cone-shaped ones that pull hot air out, let in light and move air, and trees grow up toward them. Citrus trees planted in 1906 are still alive, well past the usual 50-year lifespan. One tree was grafted to bear seven types of citrus. Forestiere never married, and his brother's family preserved the site after his death.

Second, a tapered skylight can pull hot air up and out, so it works as ventilation as well as lighting. Third, trees in protected pits seem to live longer, since they avoid frost and extreme heat. Fourth, planting hardy sour orange and grafting other citrus onto it is a sound technique for tough soil. Fifth, if you want to see the place, it's in Fresno's Highway City, right off Highway 99, and you can take a tour. Check current hours and tickets before you go.
