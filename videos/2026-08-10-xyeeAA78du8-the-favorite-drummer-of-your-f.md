## Summary

The video is a drummer's reaction to a short Sildjian session featuring JD Beck, recorded when Beck was 16 and playing with keyboardist Domi. The reactor, a professional drummer who had just finished a world tour with Residente and a UK tour with his band The Cost, calls Beck the most promising young talent in drumming. He points out Beck's unusual kit: two snares, 6-inch and 10-inch splashes, a floor tom, a 16-inch hi-hat stack and a 17-inch crash-ride. He marvels at the broken grooves, odd time signatures and fast, unpredictable stickings, which he says are nearly impossible to follow. He credits Beck, who was playing this way at 12, with popularizing the "jazzy broken groove" style now common among Instagram and jazz-vanguard drummers. The session opens with a tribute to the late Aaron Spears, which leads the reactor to promise his next video will be a reaction to Spears. He closes by saying Beck is 21 or 22 now, that he is creating a new branch of drumming, and that the performance shows skill comes from what you have "in the pocket," not from gear. He also plugs his merch and says orders get a free titanium necklace.

His main point is that a simple kit of bass drum, snare and hi-hat would sound the same in Beck's hands, so you can practice these ideas on whatever you have. These include leading with the left hand versus the right, triplets, 16ths and 32nds, and odd groupings.
