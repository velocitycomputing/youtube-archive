## Summary

John Bolton told Mattie Hail on the Trump Report that Donald Trump’s threats to impose tariffs on European allies and pursue control of Greenland are the worst move of his presidency, arguing the issue is not American or NATO security but Trump’s personal fixation. Bolton cited Trump’s message to Norway’s prime minister tying Greenland to the Nobel Peace Prize, Trump’s claim that the U.S. “psychologically” needs to own Greenland, and his attacks on the UK over returning Chagos/Diego Garcia to Mauritius despite the State Department under Marco Rubio previously supporting Keir Starmer’s deal. Bolton said the 1951 Defense of Greenland Treaty already gives the U.S. broad military access with Danish and Greenlandic consultation, making annexation unnecessary; he noted Greenland has about 57,000 people, 93 miles of paved roads, and that mineral extraction would take years. He also discussed Trump’s 10% tariff threat against eight countries starting February 1, possible 200% tariffs on French wines and champagne, a Kiel Institute study claiming 96% of tariff costs fall on American importers and consumers versus 4% on foreign sellers, Trump’s “Board of Peace” invitation to Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping with a reported $1 billion entry fee, and warnings from Jerry Baker that senior U.S. military officials could resign if ordered to invade Greenland, where NORAD operations continue at Pituffik Space Base.

Concrete research leads include the 1951 Defense of Greenland Treaty, NATO’s Arctic posture, melting-ice shipping routes, rare-mineral feasibility, the Chagos/Diego Garcia agreement, and the Kiel Institute tariff-cost methodology. Decisions to consider include asking elected representatives whether they support war-powers or tariff-authority limits, monitoring legal challenges to emergency tariff powers, and watching whether European leaders coordinate rather than split NATO. Follow-up questions: Is the U.S. seeking bases and surveillance under the existing treaty or actual sovereignty? And will Trump’s Norway/Nobel message, Board of Peace plan, and Macron/Rutte texts change policy or mainly deepen alliance uncertainty?
