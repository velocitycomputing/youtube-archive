## Summary

A carpenter suffered a severe workplace injury from a defective lift that caused chronic pain. Unable to work, he pursued workers' compensation (denied, then settled for $15,000), Social Security Disability ($18,000/year after 4.5 years of appeals), and medical marijuana—only to be dropped by his doctor for using it. Decades of fighting inadequate government safety nets, insufficient income, and bureaucratic barriers consumed him. The video traces how state and federal programs created to protect injured workers have been systematically eroded through policy changes favoring employers over workers, and how his father ultimately took his own life—leaving 694 video diaries documenting his struggle.

Push for legislative changes like Ohio's proposed paid medical and family leave bill (State Senator Blessing's), which would let injured workers recover rather than choose between health and income. Advocate for properly funded and easier-to-access workers' compensation and disability systems, healthcare advocates for civilians like those veterans receive, and cultural recognition that invisible pain is real. The core fix: safety nets should actually catch people when they fall, not force them into years of appeals and dead ends.
